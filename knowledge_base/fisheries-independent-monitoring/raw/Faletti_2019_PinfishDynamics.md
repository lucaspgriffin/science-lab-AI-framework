# Faletti et al. 2019

## Verbatim abstract

> "Forage fishes play an important role in marine ecosystems by transferring energy and nutrients through the food web. The population dynamics of forage species can therefore have cascading effects across multiple trophic levels. Here, we analyzed a 19-year dataset on Pinfish (Lagodon rhomboides) across four eastern Gulf of Mexico estuaries to investigate population dynamics, inter- and intra-annual synchrony, metapopulation portfolio effects, growth, and habitat effects. Young-of-year growth rates did not differ among estuaries. The population dynamics of these four systems were stable in the long-term, but highly dynamic inter-annually. Intra-annual dynamics were stable and predictable despite variation in long-term means. Some estuaries exhibited positive inter-annual synchrony, and all four estuaries were synchronous intra-annually. There was evidence for stronger portfolio effects for the entire four-estuary metapopulation, as well as for the two northern estuaries while the southern estuaries appeared to act as a single population. Submerged aquatic vegetation was by far the most important predictor for both presence and abundance of Pinfish. It is important to understand the factors driving forage fish population fluctuations to better predict ecosystem effects, including those to species of economic and ecological importance. These predictors can be useful for the implementation of ecosystem-based management decisions." (Abstract, p. 1 (PDF p. 1))

## Study system and design

- Species: "Pinfish (Lagodon rhomboides) are an abundant and ubiquitous marine forage fish in eastern Gulf of Mexico (eGOM) estuaries." (Introduction, p. 2 (PDF p. 2))
- Estuaries: "Apalachicola Bay (AB), Cedar Key (CK), Tampa Bay (TB), and Charlotte Harbor (CH; Fig 1)." (Methods, Sampling design, p. 3 (PDF p. 3))
- Years: "Stratified random sampling was conducted monthly from 1998-2016" (Methods, Sampling design, p. 3 (PDF p. 3))
- Dataset length: "we analyzed a 19-year dataset on Pinfish (Lagodon rhomboides) across four eastern Gulf of Mexico estuaries" (Abstract, p. 1 (PDF p. 1))
- Objectives: "1) analyze the long-term, inter- and intra-annual population dynamics of Pinfish across these estuaries to describe the spatial and regional patterns in long-term mean and variation, 2) investigate the inter- and intra-annual population synchrony among these four estuaries, and measure the strength of metapopulation portfolio effects, 3) analyze regional variation in YOY size and growth, and determine if this variation influenced the density-biomass dynamics within each estuary, and 4) model the habitat variables that were related to long-term Pinfish population dynamics." (Introduction, pp. 2-3 (PDF pp. 2-3))
- Sample sizes per month: "Mean number of seine hauls per month varied between 9-20 per estuary (See S1 Table)." (Methods, Sampling design, p. 3 (PDF p. 3))
- Length-weight data: "The regression parameters (a and b) were derived from estuary-specific length and weight data from subsamples of Pinfish caught during monthly inshore fisheries-independent surveys (2001-2017; n = 3,517)." (Methods, Analysis, p. 3 (PDF p. 3))
- Length-weight model: "W = a x SL^b" with "W is weight in grams, SL is standard length in millimeters, and a and b are length-weight constants." (Methods, Analysis, p. 3 (PDF p. 3); equation as printed in Eq 1)
- Subsampling: "All Pinfish were counted and a subsample of up to ten randomly selected individuals was measured for standard length (SL) in millimeters (mm) before being released alive." (Methods, Sampling design, p. 3 (PDF p. 3))
- Ethics: "All animals counted and measured for this dataset were released alive in the field." (Methods, Sampling design, p. 3 (PDF p. 3))
- Life history context: "Juvenile Pinfish settle in Florida seagrass beds from November to March [20]." (Introduction, p. 2 (PDF p. 2))
- Maturation: "Following maturation (~110mm) [25], adult Pinfish migrate offshore to spawn between October-May [26, 27, 28, 29]" (Introduction, p. 2 (PDF p. 2))
- Statistical software: "Analyses were conducted using the MASS and car packages for R Statistical Computing Environment [52, 53, 54]." (Methods, Analysis, p. 6 (PDF p. 6))
- Other packages cited: modes version 0.7.0 for the bimodality coefficient and diptest version 0.75-7 for Hartigan's dip test (references 49 and 51, p. 17 (PDF p. 17)).
- Data deposit: "All of the data underlying the results presented in the study have been made available here: https://doi.org/10.17882/61775." (Data Availability Statement, p. 1 (PDF p. 1))
- Funding: "Funding for this research was provided by a fellowship from the Forage Fish Research Program (FFRP), which is a collaboration between the Florida Forage Fish Coalition, the Florida Fish and Wildlife Research Institute, the Pew Charitable Trusts, the International Game Fish Association, and Florida academics." (Funding statement, pp. 1-2 (PDF pp. 1-2))

## FIM data use

### Program and design

- Source program: "Specimen collections were conducted via standard protocols by the Florida Fish and Wildlife Research Institute (FWRI) Fisheries Independent Monitoring (FIM) program." (Methods, Sampling design, p. 3 (PDF p. 3))
- Authorisation: "These protocols were authorized by the Florida Fish and Wildlife Conservation Commission for state fisheries and conservation research." (Methods, Sampling design, p. 3 (PDF p. 3))
- Design and years: "Stratified random sampling was conducted monthly from 1998-2016 by the FWRI FIM program in each of four estuaries: Apalachicola Bay (AB), Cedar Key (CK), Tampa Bay (TB), and Charlotte Harbor (CH; Fig 1)." (Methods, Sampling design, p. 3 (PDF p. 3))
- Strata: "Strata were defined by spatial zones (based on geographic, hydrologic, and logistical criteria), water depths, and habitat types (submerged aquatic vegetation [SAV], unvegetated, and shoreline)." (Methods, Sampling design, p. 3 (PDF p. 3))
- Grid selection: "Each estuary was subdivided into 1 nautical mile2 (nmi2) grids and stratified by depth." (Methods, Sampling design, p. 3 (PDF p. 3))
- Microgrid: "Within each randomly selected grid, a 183 m2 microgrid was randomly selected as the starting point to search for the designated habitat [44]." (Methods, Sampling design, p. 3 (PDF p. 3)); reference 44 is the McMichael 2009 FIM 2008 annual data summary report (p. 17 (PDF p. 17)).
- Map: "Blue polygons represent areas sampled by FWRI's FIM program from 1998-2016 in four eastern Gulf of Mexico (eGOM) estuaries" (Fig 1 caption, p. 4 (PDF p. 4))

### Gear

- Gear: "Sampling was conducted using a 21.3 m center-bag seine with 3.2 mm mesh netting that is restricted to use in depths <1.8m." (Methods, Sampling design, p. 3 (PDF p. 3))
- Grid depth filter: "Therefore, grids with a minimum depth between 0.1 and 1.8 m were randomly selected within each estuary for sampling." (Methods, Sampling design, p. 3 (PDF p. 3))
- Haul geometry: "A seine haul consisted of pulling the seine 9.1 m with a 15.5 m line maintaining a consistent net opening, resulting in a sampled area of 140 m2." (Methods, Sampling design, p. 3 (PDF p. 3))
- Retrieval: "After net deployment, the sample was retrieved by pulling the leads around a pole to concentrate the sample in the bag." (Methods, Sampling design, p. 3 (PDF p. 3))
- Covariates measured at each site: "At each sampling site, environmental variables including salinity and water temperature (degrees C) were measured with a YSI handheld multiparameter water quality meter, and the percent cover of SAV was visually estimated in ten percent increments." (Methods, Sampling design, p. 3 (PDF p. 3); the source prints the degree symbol as a separate glyph)
- Depth covariate: water depth was an explanatory variable in the models (Methods, Analysis, p. 5 (PDF p. 5)); the measurement method for depth is not stated in the text.

### Subset kept

- Shoreline filter: "Seine hauls conducted a minimum of 5m away from the shoreline were used in these analyses." (Methods, Analysis, p. 3 (PDF p. 3))
- Only the 21.3 m seine is analysed; no other FIM gear (haul seine, trawl) is used.
- Months used for growth: "growth rates were approximated with mean SL data with an emphasis on months April through July to reduce biases related to settlement (January-March) and ontogenetic movements of juveniles (August-December)." (Methods, Analysis, p. 5 (PDF p. 5))
- Species: Pinfish only; all months of 1998-2016 are used for the density and biomass series.

### Standardisation

- Density: "catch abundances were converted to densities by dividing the number of Pinfish collected in each sampling event by the total area sampled with the gear." (Methods, Analysis, p. 3 (PDF p. 3))
- Unit: "density (fish/100m2) for each net haul was calculated and then averaged over all months and years for each estuary." (Methods, Analysis, p. 3 (PDF p. 3))
- Inter-annual series: "Second, mean density was calculated over all months in each estuary per year to examine inter-annual patterns." (Methods, Analysis, p. 3 (PDF p. 3))
- Intra-annual series: "Third, density was averaged over years in each estuary per month to explore intra-annual patterns." (Methods, Analysis, p. 3 (PDF p. 3))
- Biomass: "Mean biomass (g/100m2) was estimated to compare dynamics between Pinfish density and biomass. Lengths of the measured subsample of Pinfish were extrapolated to the remaining unmeasured Pinfish sample before biomass was calculated using the length-weight relationship" (Methods, Analysis, p. 3 (PDF p. 3))
- The index is a mean of per-haul density, not a delta-lognormal or GLM or GAM standardised index. No year, month or site effects are fitted to build the index.
- Detrending for synchrony: "To investigate inter-annual synchrony, the density data were detrended to remove seasonal signals. For intra-annual synchrony, analyses were focused only on the seasonal signals." (Methods, Analysis, p. 4 (PDF p. 4))
- Peak series: "The dynamics in peak inter-annual density (three-month moving average) were relatively similar in AB (CV = 0.73), TB (CV = 0.63), and CH (CV = 0.77)." (Results, Metapopulation portfolio effects, p. 7 (PDF p. 7))

### Zeros

- "A zero-altered negative binomial (ZANB) analysis was used to explore which environmental variables were related to the observed variation in Pinfish presence and density for each individual estuary. A ZANB model was selected due to overdispersion of both zeroes and non-zero data." (Methods, Analysis, p. 5 (PDF p. 5))
- Zeros are handled in the habitat model by the binomial presence-absence component; the density and biomass series average all hauls, so zero hauls enter the means (the text does not say so explicitly).

### Model structure

- Candidate families: "Four methods were tested (zero-inflated negative binomial, zero-inflated Poisson, and zero-altered Poisson). Akaike Information Criteria (AIC) model selection was used to determine the most appropriate model. The ZANB approach was the most appropriate, with higher R2 and lower AIC than the other three models." (Methods, Analysis, p. 5 (PDF p. 5))
- Components: "This analysis consists of two generalized linear models: a binomial distribution was used to model presence-absence probability, and a negative binomial distribution was used to model density patterns." (Methods, Analysis, p. 5 (PDF p. 5))
- Covariates: "Explanatory variables included percent cover of SAV, water temperature, salinity, water depth, SAV2 (to account for non-linear saturation effects of habitat structure), and SAV-temperature interaction term." (Methods, Analysis, p. 5 (PDF p. 5); the source prints the interaction as SAV followed by an asterisk-like glyph and temperature)
- Selection: "A stepwise model selection was conducted via a combination of forward addition and backward elimination on both the presence-absence and abundance models for each estuary based on minimization of the AIC." (Methods, Analysis, p. 5 (PDF p. 5))
- Collinearity: "At a tolerance level of 0.5, there was no evidence of multicollinearity among any of the predictors in both the presence-absence and density models for all estuaries (VIF range: 1.001-1.352)" (Methods, Analysis, p. 6 (PDF p. 6))
- Spatial and temporal terms: the model has no spatial or temporal random effects, no year term, no month term and no zone term. It is fitted separately for each estuary.
- Software: "Analyses were conducted using the MASS and car packages for R Statistical Computing Environment [52, 53, 54]." (Methods, Analysis, p. 6 (PDF p. 6))

### Portfolio effects

- Variability of peak density: "The dynamics in peak inter-annual density (three-month moving average) were relatively similar in AB (CV = 0.73), TB (CV = 0.63), and CH (CV = 0.77). Density dynamics were more variable in CK (CV = 1.12)." (Results, p. 7 (PDF p. 7))
- Variability of peak biomass: "Specifically, they were similar in AB (CV = 0.49), TB (CV = 0.42), and CH (CV = 0.44), but more variable in CK (CV = 0.88)." (Results, p. 7 (PDF p. 7))
- Full portfolio: "The full, four-estuary portfolio effect was 42% more stable than a single homogenous population (CVPE = 1.42; Fig 5)." (Results, p. 7 (PDF p. 7))
- Northern and southern: "The portfolio effects differed between the northern (CVPE = 1.35) and southern (CVPE = 1.05) metapopulations (Fig 5)." (Results, p. 7 (PDF p. 7))
- Definition of the metric: "Larger values represent stronger portfolio effects, or greater variance dampening, while low values represent no variance dampening or complete synchrony [47]. A CVPE = 1.0 therefore effectively represents a single homogeneous population while CVPE >1 represents a population with asynchronous dynamics that is more stable than a single homogeneous population." (Methods, Analysis, p. 5 (PDF p. 5))

## Methods worth reusing

- Density per haul: "density (fish/100m2) for each net haul was calculated and then averaged over all months and years for each estuary." (Methods, Analysis, p. 3 (PDF p. 3))
- Distance-from-shore filter: "Seine hauls conducted a minimum of 5m away from the shoreline were used in these analyses." (Methods, Analysis, p. 3 (PDF p. 3))
- Seasonal and trend separation: "To investigate inter-annual synchrony, the density data were detrended to remove seasonal signals. For intra-annual synchrony, analyses were focused only on the seasonal signals. Then cross-correlations were conducted for each estuary pair to test for inter- and intra-annual synchrony." (Methods, Analysis, p. 4 (PDF p. 4))
- Lag window: Table 1 caption: "Cross correlation coefficients (r) of peak synchrony measured within 0-3 month lags between estuaries" (Table 1 caption, p. 7 (PDF p. 7))
- Portfolio effect formula: "For each portfolio, we calculated the average coefficient of variation portfolio effect (CVPE) [48]" with CVPE equal to the mean of the local coefficients of variation divided by the coefficient of variation of the whole metapopulation (Methods, Analysis, p. 5 (PDF p. 5), Eq 2); reference 48 is Anderson, Cooper and Dulvy 2013.
- Cohort split: "This smaller cohort was split into a 'month 0' to represent the recruitment of smaller fish for the beginning of the following year." (Methods, Analysis, p. 5 (PDF p. 5))
- Growth model: "growth was estimated with the model: ln(Lt) = ln(L0) + G x t, where G is the instantaneous growth rate (per month), Lt is the monthly mean SL (mm), L0 the theoretical SL at which Pinfish recruit to each estuary, and t is time in months." (Methods, Analysis, p. 5 (PDF p. 5), Eq 3; the source prints the model across a line break)
- Growth-model assumption: "The assessment of growth coefficients in each estuary assumed limited migration of Pinfish among estuaries, however, other factors could affect size class structure such as size selective mortality and egress." (Methods, Analysis, p. 5 (PDF p. 5))
- Habitat model: zero-altered negative binomial fitted per estuary with SAV, temperature, salinity, depth, SAV2 and an SAV by temperature term, with stepwise AIC selection on both components (full quotes under FIM data use, Model structure).
- Family comparison by AIC: four families compared and ZANB retained "with higher R2 and lower AIC than the other three models." (Methods, Analysis, p. 5 (PDF p. 5))
- Collinearity check: tolerance test at 0.5, VIF range 1.001-1.352 (Methods, Analysis, pp. 5-6 (PDF pp. 5-6)).

## Estuary trends

- Long-term stability: "Although Pinfish density and biomass were highly dynamic in the region, the populations were stable across these four systems in the long-term, despite the occurrences of severe stressors such as severe cold events [62], red tide events [63], and seagrass die-offs [64]." (Discussion, p. 12 (PDF p. 12))
- Absence of trend: "The long-term stability of the Pinfish population is evident in the lack of trends in both density and biomass in this 19-year dataset" (Discussion, p. 12 (PDF p. 12)). No trend test or slope is reported in the Methods or Results.
- North and south: "Southern estuaries (CH and TB) had far greater density and biomass than northern estuaries (CK and AB)." (Discussion, p. 12 (PDF p. 12))
- Cedar Key: "The larger SLs found in the CK region may have been due to density dependent growth, as this estuary was found to have the lowest densities, possibly reducing the strength of intraspecific competition and allowing Pinfish to grow larger." (Discussion, p. 13 (PDF p. 13))
- Apalachicola Bay: "Apalachicola Bay (AB) also had the second lowest density, suggesting it may have had lower habitat quality or fewer trophic resources for Pinfish compared to the other estuaries." (Discussion, p. 13 (PDF p. 13))
- Larval supply, Cedar Key: "The CK estuary (i.e., the greater Big Bend region) is located inshore of a very wide portion of the shelf, so larvae may have longer distances to travel to reach this estuary compared to the other estuaries." (Discussion, p. 12 (PDF p. 12))
- Upwelling and recruitment: "In years when nearshore waters of the TB and CH regions-and AB to a similar but lesser degree-are renewed during protracted upwelling circulation, conditions are not favorable for transport to the CK region [59], and vice-versa." (Discussion, p. 12 (PDF p. 12))
- Portfolio interpretation: "In contrast, the low PE value for the southern component suggested that CH and TB were acting as a single population during the study period. This was further supported and explained by the strong population synchrony between the southernmost estuaries." (Discussion, p. 12 (PDF p. 12))
- Alternative reading: "Alternatively, the stability of the metapopulation across the four estuaries could have resulted from the metapopulation being well-mixed and acting as a single population." (Discussion, p. 12 (PDF p. 12))
- Regional seagrass: "regional variation in dominant seagrass has been observed, with higher cover of turtle grass (Thalassia testudinum) in the south and manatee grass (Syringodium filiforme) in the north [30]." (Discussion, p. 12 (PDF p. 12))
- Temperature and seasonal drop: "It is important to note that Pinfish abundance was significantly negatively correlated with water temperature. The increase in Pinfish density during settlement, and decrease during egress, both coincide with lower water temperatures." (Discussion, p. 13 (PDF p. 13))
- Winter temperature: "Higher winter temperatures in southern estuaries may be more conducive to survival and growth of newly settled juveniles, compared to northern estuaries." (Discussion, p. 13 (PDF p. 13))

## Stated limitations

- Growth assumption: "The assessment of growth coefficients in each estuary assumed limited migration of Pinfish among estuaries, however, other factors could affect size class structure such as size selective mortality and egress." (Methods, Analysis, p. 5 (PDF p. 5))
- Bimodality coefficient: "Due to the potential for the bimodality coefficient to incorrectly assume a bimodal distribution from a highly skewed unimodal distribution [50], we also conducted the Hartigan's dip test [51]" (Methods, Analysis, p. 5 (PDF p. 5))
- Gear susceptibility: "An alternative explanation is that the habitats that Pinfish used in CK left them more susceptible to the sampling gears at larger lengths than in the other systems." (Discussion, p. 13 (PDF p. 13))
- Cause of late-season decline: "post-settlement mortality, escapement from sampling gear by larger individuals, or perhaps offshore movement in preparation for spawning [26]" are given as possible explanations that the data cannot separate (Discussion, p. 13 (PDF p. 13)).
- Portfolio ambiguity: the authors give two explanations for the portfolio result, asynchronous dynamics or a single well-mixed population (Discussion, p. 12 (PDF p. 12), quoted above under Estuary trends).
- Synchrony cause: "This stability could also be attributed to changes in environmental attributes causing populations to fluctuate similarly, as correlation among the separate populations of the same species can result from correlation among the variables present [65] and result in synchronization." (Discussion, p. 12 (PDF p. 12))
- Habitat effect interpretation: the saturating SAV effect is offered as "it is possible that Pinfish avoid these areas in order to maximize foraging efficiency within low density seagrass habitats" (Discussion, p. 13 (PDF p. 13)); no test is made.
- Variables not in the models: the explanation for between-estuary density differences is given as "possibly due to variation in the quality of suitable juvenile habitat (i.e., seagrass coverage, suitable temperature and salinity ranges) or differences in larval supply." (Discussion, p. 12 (PDF p. 12))
- Seagrass threat: "Seagrass habitats are already influenced by a tremendous number of anthropogenic stressors including nutrient input, habitat destruction [90, 91], and increasing water temperatures from climate change which may lead to severe declines in seagrass growth and survival [92]." (Conclusion, p. 14 (PDF p. 14))

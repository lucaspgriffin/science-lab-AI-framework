## Verbatim abstract

The report has no abstract. The Executive Summary is reproduced instead.

> "This report assesses the status of Red Drum populations found in the four regions along the Atlantic and Gulf coasts of Florida. The regions were defined by Florida counties such that the northwest region includes waters off Escambia through Pasco counties, the southwest region includes Pinellas through Monroe counties; southeast region is Miami-Dade through Volusia counties, and northeast region is Flagler through Nassau counties. Red Drum population dynamics are described for the period 1989-2019 utilizing all information on catch, effort, relative abundance, and size/age composition" (Executive Summary, p. 2 (PDF p. 2))

> "Trends in the recreational total catch rates (A+B1+B2, number fish/angler hour) from the MRIP intercept data indicate that there has been a general increase in CPUE of Red Drum in the NE, NW, and SE regions since early the 1990s. In the SW region, total catch rates were variable but have remained stable since the early 1990s." (Executive Summary, p. 2 (PDF p. 2))

> "The relative abundance indices generated for young-of-the-year Red Drum were variable with no significant trend in all four regions, with recent peaks in the NW (2014, 2018-2019), SW (2014-2018), NE (2013), and SE (2017)." (Executive Summary, p. 2 (PDF p. 2)) "decreasing trend over the timeseries in the NE, and show significant recent declines (2015-2019) in the SE." (Executive Summary, p. 3 (PDF p. 3))

> "Stock synthesis models (NW, SW, NE, SE) were developed to assess the status of Red Drum populations in Florida waters. Models were run from 1989 to 2019, accounted for 41 ages (0-40+), and were fit to catch, CPUE indices, length composition, and size-at-age data. Model diagnostics demonstrated convergence on a global solution. Discernable retrospective patterns were observed for the SE model only. Fits to the datasets from a parametric bootstrap analysis were adequate for all regions as most base run estimated parameters and derived quantities were inside the central range of the estimates produced by the bootstrap analysis." (Executive Summary, p. 3 (PDF p. 3))

> "Overall fishing mortality rate estimates for Red Drum ages 1-5 have remained at fairly low levels since the late 1980’s in all four regions. However, recent increases have been apparent in the NE from 2010-2019 and the SE from 2015-2018." (Executive Summary, p. 3 (PDF p. 3))

> "Biomass estimates of Red Drum show generally increasing trends since the early 1990s in all four regions, likely in response to reduced fishing mortalities. Current spawning stock biomass (SSBcurrent, mt) calculated as the geometric mean of the past three years, is estimated to be 14,562 in the NW, 14,431 in the SW, 17,163 in the NE, and 27,940 in the SE region." (Executive Summary, p. 3 (PDF p. 3))

> "The fishing mortality resulting in an SPR of 35% (FSPR35%) was estimated to be 0.23, 0.24, 0.26, and 0.23 in the NW, SW, NE, and SE regions, respectively. The spawning stock biomass (mt) when the population is at SPR of 35% (SSBSPR35%) was estimated to be 6,892 in the NW, 9,013 in the SW, 7,801 in the NE, and 10,336 in the SE." (Executive Summary, p. 3 (PDF p. 3))

> "Ratios of Bcurrent/BSPR35% and Fcurrent/FSPR35% from four assessment regions indicate that Red Drum are currently neither overfished nor undergoing overfishing in Florida. The Bcurrent/BSPR35% ratios for the past three years were 2.1, 1.6, 2.2, and 2.7 in the NW, SW, NE, and SE regions, respectively. The Fcurrent/FSPR35% ratios were 0.7, 0.2, 0.5, and 0.8 in the NW, SW, NE, SE regions, respectively." (Executive Summary, p. 3 (PDF p. 3))

> "Estimates of current escapement rates (geometric mean of the last 3 years, 2017-2019) in the NW, SW, and NE regions exceeded the Commission’s target of 40%. Although the SE region of Florida is exceeding the escapement rate management target in the terminal year (2019) of the assessment (55%), it does not meet the current escapement rate management target. Current escapement rates for 2017-2019 were 48% in the NW, 72% in the SW, 61% in the NE, and 35% in the SE region." (Executive Summary, p. 3 (PDF p. 3))

## Study system and design

- Species and regions: "This report assesses the status of Red Drum populations found in the four regions along the Atlantic and Gulf coasts of Florida." (Executive Summary, p. 2 (PDF p. 2))
- Management target: "the Commission’s management target of 35% SPR and 40% escapement rate" (Section 5, p. 34 (PDF p. 34))
- Time span: "The models were run from 1989 to 2019." (Section 4.1.2.1, p. 21 (PDF p. 21))
- Model software: "Stock Synthesis (v 3.3) allows for dome shaped retention which is necessary to model slot limit fisheries" (Section 4.1.2.1, p. 21 (PDF p. 21))
- Model structure: "For all regions, the models contained one sex, one season, one growth pattern, two fleets (commercial and recreational), and three surveys (MRIP, FIM YOY, and FIM sub-adult indices)." (Section 4.1.2.1, p. 21 (PDF p. 21))
- Areas: "All regional models were configured to have two areas (area 1 = inshore, area 2 = offshore) to estimate unidirectional movement (Area 1 to Area 2) by age." (Section 4.1.2.1, p. 21 (PDF p. 21))
- Ages: "All models account for ages 0-40+." (Section 4.1.2.1, p. 21 (PDF p. 21))
- Bins: "Two-centimeter size bins were used for length composition and conditional age data." (Section 4.1.2.1, p. 21 (PDF p. 21))
- Recreational discards: "recreational discard numbers were input into the model and retention was estimated using a discard mortality rate of 0.08." (Section 4.1.2.1, p. 21 (PDF p. 21))
- Growth: "Red Drum growth was modeled using the von Bertalanffy equation and parameters were estimated within the SS model." (Section 4.1.2.1, p. 21 (PDF p. 21))
- Maturity: "A maturity vector was input to the model and assumed to be the same in all regions with approximately 50% mature at age 3 and 100% mature by age 4." (Section 4.1.2.1, p. 22 (PDF p. 22))
- Stock-recruit: "The Beverton-Holt equation was used for the spawner-recruit function." (Section 4.1.2.1, p. 22 (PDF p. 22))
- Fishing mortality summary: "summary instantaneous fishing mortality (F) was estimated as the N-weighted average for ages 1-5 (the most vulnerable ages)" (Section 4.1.2.1, p. 22 (PDF p. 22))
- Escapement: "annual escapement was calculated as the exponent of the negative sum of age- specific fishing mortality rates in each year, 𝐸𝑡=exp(−Σ𝐹𝑡), for ages 0-4 in this assessment." (Section 4.1.2.1, p. 23 (PDF p. 23))
- Management units: "Due to the need for regional assessments, within the 2011 and 2015 stock assessments Florida was divided into four regions, northern and southern on each coast (Murphy 2011, Chagaris et al. 2015)." (Section 1.3, p. 8 (PDF p. 8))

### Cited comparison values

- Region to estuary mapping: "The Northwest region included data from Apalachicola Bay and Cedar Key, the Southwest region included Tampa Bay and Charlotte Harbor data, the Northeast region included data from the St. John River and coastal tributaries, and the Southeast region included northern and Southern Indian River Lagoon samples." ### Young-of-the-year index

- Gear and size class: "Annual region-specific young-of-the-year (red drum smaller than or equal to 40 mm standard length during a ‘recruitment window’) indices were based on collections of Red Drum made using 21.3-m center-bag seines." (Section 3.1, p. 18 (PDF p. 18))
- Season: "Sets used to develop the regional indices were made during the peak recruitment season of September-March." (Section 3.1, p. 18 (PDF p. 18))
- Length handling: "Up to 20 fish within a size class (e.g. small or large) were measured for standard length (SL) and all were counted. When more than 20 Red Drum were encountered then length frequencies of the 20 fish were expanded to the total number caught to estimate the sample-catch length frequency." (Section 3.1, p. 18 (PDF p. 18))
- Definition and year assignment: "Young-of-the-year were defined as Red Drum captured during the period September through March that were smaller than 40 mm SL, those caught after September were assigned to following year." (Section 3.2, p. 18 (PDF p. 18))
- Wording difference noted: Section 3.1 says "smaller than or equal to 40 mm standard length" and Section 3.2 says "smaller than 40 mm SL" (p. 18).
- Gear used for index: "Indices of relative abundance were developed for YOY age-0 fish using the 21.3-m seine and for sub-adults using the 183-m seine." (Section 3.2, p. 18 (PDF p. 18))

### Sub-adult index

- Gear and size class: "Region-specific indices for sub-adult Red Drum (300 mm SL and larger) were based on year-round collections made using the 183-m seines deployed following the fishery-independent survey design (Fisheries-Independent Monitoring staff 2008)." (Section 3.1, p. 18 (PDF p. 18))
- Size filter: "Red Drum smaller than 30 cm SL were excluded prior to developing the sub-adult index." (Section 3.1, p. 18 (PDF p. 18))
- Repeated in indices section: "Fish less than 30 cm SL were excluded from the sub-adult index." (Section 3.2, p. 18 (PDF p. 18))
- Handling in the field: "In the 183-m haul seine survey, all captured Red Drum were measured for length and if five or fewer Red Drum were captured these were brought back to the lab for full biological workup, including age determination." (Section 3.1, p. 18 (PDF p. 18))
- Size range captured: "On the Atlantic coast, most Red Drum were between 6 cm (2.4 inches TL) and 80 cm (31.5 inches TL) in the NE region and between 6 cm (2.4 inches TL) and 118 cm (46.5 inches TL) in the SE region. On the Gulf coast, most Red Drum captured using this gear were 6-90 cm (2.4-35.4 inches TL) in the SW region and were 6-100 cm (2.4-39.4 inches TL) in the NW region." (Section 3.1, p. 18 (PDF p. 18))
- Reader caution: the 6 cm lower bound in that passage is stated for the length-frequency description of the full 183-m catch; the index itself excludes fish under 30 cm SL (p. 18).

### Standardisation

- Pre-standardisation subset: "Prior to standardization, the data were subset to remove any months, zones, or strata that rarely encountered Red Drum." (Section 3.2, p. 18 (PDF p. 18))
- Method: "Like the MRFSS/MRIP index, the delta method (Lo et al. 1992) was used to standardize the FIM indices." (Section 3.2, p. 18 (PDF p. 18))
- Model components: "The response variable in the positive model is the total catch in each set assuming a gamma distribution with a log-link and the response variable in the binomial model was 1 (Red Drum caught) or 0." (Section 3.2, p. 18 (PDF p. 18))
- Candidate covariates: "Potential explanatory" (Section 3.2, p. 18 (PDF p. 18)) "variables included year, month, bottom vegetation, bottom type, shore type, bay zone, temperature, and salinity." (Section 3.2, p. 19 (PDF p. 19))
- Delta method as defined for the recreational index: "This method uses two generalized linear models (GLMs), one to model the number of Red Drum caught on positive trips only (those capturing Red Drum), and a binomial model to estimate the probability of capturing a Red Drum on a trip. The final index is the product of the two GLMs." (Section 2.3.6, p. 17 (PDF p. 17))
- Covariate selection described for the recreational index: "Significant variables were identified using stepwise forward-selection that started with a null model (including year only) and added the most significant terms sequentially until there was no further improvement to the Bayes Information Criteria" (Section 2.3.6, p. 17 (PDF p. 17)). The report does not state that the same selection rule was applied to the FIM indices.
- Zeros: zero-catch sets enter the binomial component ("1 (Red Drum caught) or 0", p. 18). The report gives no separate zero-handling rule.
- Index columns: Tables 9 and 10 list "N", "N pos", "Obs Mean", "Index (scaled to mean)" and "Index SE (log)" by year and region (p. 50-53).
- Figures 17 and 18 caption: "Values above each boxplot represent the number of trips that caught Red Drum." (Figures 17 and 18, p. 74-77 (PDF p. 74-77))

## Methods worth reusing

- Delta method standardisation for FIM seine indices: "the delta method (Lo et al. 1992) was used to standardize the FIM indices. The response variable in the positive model is the total catch in each set assuming a gamma distribution with a log-link and the response variable in the binomial model was 1 (Red Drum caught) or 0." (Section 3.2, p. 18 (PDF p. 18))
- Candidate covariates for the FIM model: "year, month, bottom vegetation, bottom type, shore type, bay zone, temperature, and salinity" (Section 3.2, p. 19 (PDF p. 19))
- Regional pooling of estuaries into a regional index follows the mapping quoted under FIM data use (Section 3.2, p. 18).
- Index error: Tables 9 and 10 report "Index SE (log)" for each year (p. 50-53). The report does not state how the standard error was obtained for the FIM indices; for the recreational index, "Final year-specific estimates and distributions of total catch rate were determined from a Monte Carlo simulation using 10,000 randomly chosen normal deviates applied to the estimated standard deviations and least square means." - Mapping an index to a survey in Stock Synthesis: "FIM YOY selectivity was specified as fully selected for age 0, and FIM haul seine selectivity was specified as double logistic (dome shaped selectivity)." (Section 4.1.2.1, p. 23 (PDF p. 23))
- Composition weighting: "Iterative reweighting was performed according to Francis composition weighting method TA1.8 (Francis 2011) until most datasets stabilized to the values presented in Table 11" (Section 4.1.2.2, p. 23 (PDF p. 23))
- Convergence test: "the starting values of all active parameters were “jittered” by adding a random normal deviate multiplied by 20% of the defined parameter range." (Section 4.1.2.3, p. 23 (PDF p. 23))

## Stated limitations

### FIM data and sampling

- Estuary and juvenile focus: "Because fishery independent surveys and the recreational fishery primarily target juveniles and sub-adults within estuary and bay systems before their ontogenetic shift to offshore spawning stocks, assessment models are largely uninformed on older spawning adults." (Section 7, p. 36 (PDF p. 36))
- Regional data gaps: "Recent fishery independent data collection has increased our confidence to assess the Southwest region spatially and estimate movement parameters within the model, however the other regional models are data poor in this regard and are assumed to have movement estimates similar to those estimated by the Southwest model." (Section 7, p. 36 (PDF p. 36))
- Age data: "Regional growth estimates used as initial parameters within models were constructed from age data collected within each respective region. However, the Northwest and Northeast regional age data were lacking in older ages (Figure 1), this likely resulted in estimated lower Linf (asymptotic or theoretical maximum length) and higher K von Bertalanffy growth parameters." (Section 4.1.4, p. 33 (PDF p. 33))
- Age-length keys: "the age-length keys developed mostly from scientific survey sampling are similar to age-length keys that should be derived from samples made from the angler landings." (Section 7, p. 35 (PDF p. 35))
- Pooling: "increased across-year pooling of length samples when computing the age- composition information, and the need to share coast-wide age-length keys between regions." (Section 7, p. 35-36 (PDF p. 35-36))
- Data-weight instability: "It should be noted that in all regions during iterative reweighting, some conditional length at age datasets did not fully stabilize (less than 1% change from previous iteration) and continued to decrease to zero if enough iterations (8-15+) were performed and no more suggested tuning to all datasets could be applied." (Section 4.1.2.2, p. 23 (PDF p. 23))
- Index-model mismatch, NE: the model prediction for the sub-adult index "shows a flat or slightly increasing trajectory" while the observed index declines (Section 4.1.3.3, p. 28 (PDF p. 28)).
- Flat YOY fits: model YOY predictions "remained quite flat" in the SW and NE (Sections 4.1.3.2 and 4.1.3.3, p. 26 and 28).

### Model and assessment

- Growth: "Red drum grow extremely rapidly as juveniles and then grow very little as adults, making the von Bertalanffy curve inadequate to accurately describe their growth (Porch et al. 2002)." (Section 4.1.4, p. 33 (PDF p. 33))
- Discard mortality: "We assumed, a priori, a discard mortality rate of 8% based on some older short-term studies and an approximated size composition for the earlier time period." (Section 4.1.4, p. 33 (PDF p. 33))
- Biomass caution: "Spawning stock biomass estimates from base models should be considered with caution." (Section 4.1.4, p. 33 (PDF p. 33))
- Cryptic biomass: "model dynamics may be prone to build up cryptic biomass in older ages which are less “available” to the fishery and experience little to no fishing mortality." (Section 4.1.4, p. 33 (PDF p. 33))
- Southwest 2019 discards: "This high value (5.8 million fish) resulted in the model having poor fits to indices and very high recruitment in 2018. Better model fits were achieved by using the average discard value from 2016-2018 (1.5 million fish) in place for 2019." (Section 4.1.3.2, p. 26 (PDF p. 26))
- Red tide: "FWC does not have any reliable quantitative data to incorporate into these models currently, as the extent to which red tides affect populations of ecologically and economically important fisheries over temporal and spatial scales is poorly known." (Section 7, p. 36 (PDF p. 36))
- Environmental events: "There is a need to investigate ways to incorporate environmental episodic events (e.g. red tide, cold kills), and effectively quantify mortality from fish kills." (Section 7, p. 36 (PDF p. 36))
- Environmental covariates and recruitment (from the 2015 recommendations repeated in Appendix 11.4): "A need for a better understanding of spatial and temporal dynamics of Red Drum spawning populations, including investigations of environmental covariates related to recruitment." (Appendix 11.4, p. 129 (PDF p. 129))
- Age and size sampling: "There continues to be insufficient sampling of the size and age structure of live-released Red Drum and too few age-samples collected directly from the recreational fishery landings." (Section 7, p. 35 (PDF p. 35))

# Walters et al. 2013

## Verbatim abstract

> "A passive acoustic survey using a random stratified design detected spawning aggregations of sand seatrout Cynoscion arenarius over 2 sequential spawning seasons (2004 and 2005) in Tampa Bay, Florida. In 2005, an intense Karenia brevis red tide at ichthyotoxic concentrations entered Tampa Bay 3 mo after the spawning season began. The bloom persisted through the end of the spawning season and was temporally associated with significant changes in the spatial distribution of spawning aggregations. Red tide was most prevalent and concentrated within the lower portion of Tampa Bay and extended to a lesser degree into the middle bay, but remained absent from the upper bay. While the percentage of sand seatrout aggregations in the middle bay zone did not significantly change from 2004 to 2005, aggregations in the non-impacted area of the upper bay increased, and the red tide-exposed lower bay experienced a significant and pronounced decline. These significant bay-wide changes in the sand seatrout spawning population coincided with the red tide event, most notably the considerable decline in the lower bay, but the broad spatial distribution of the aggregations in concert with certain reproductive and life history characteristics may buffer the population from long-term effects. Typically, it is difficult to assess the effect of disturbances on marine fish populations due to complexities in measuring the extent of the perturbation and the magnitude of the loss to the population. Our spatially explicit sampling design further enabled us to demonstrate the widespread effects of red tide on fisheries and provides an important tool for assessing the extent of loss to a spawning fish population." (Abstract, p. 191 (PDF p. 1))

## Study system and design

The paper holds two data streams. The primary one is the authors' own passive acoustic hydrophone survey (not FIM data). The second is the FIM juvenile sand seatrout abundance series shown in Fig. 7 and the FIM sampling universe used to lay out the hydrophone grids. Both are listed below and labelled.

- Species: "sand seatrout Cynoscion arenarius" in "Tampa Bay, Florida" (Abstract, p. 191 (PDF p. 1)).
- Years and season: "Sampling was conducted from the first week of April through the last week of September in 2004 and 2005, from approximately 20:00 h EDT (sunset) to 01:00 h EDT." (Sand seatrout hydrophone sampling, p. 193 (PDF p. 3))
- Why the season and time were chosen: "Seasonal and diel sampling periodicities for this study were dictated by the spawning behavior of spotted seatrout Cynoscion nebulosus, a congener of sand seatrout and the original focus of the passive acoustic survey." (p. 193 (PDF p. 3))
- Zones: "Tampa Bay was divided into 3 zones based on geographic and logistical criteria: Zone 1 (upper bay), Zone 2 (middle bay), and Zone 3 (lower bay)." (p. 193 (PDF p. 3))
- Grid design: "Each zone was composed of 1 nautical mile-square grids (Fig. 1). Only grids containing water >1.5 m depth in ≥5% of the grid area were included in the sampling design (n = 285)." (p. 193 (PDF p. 3))
- Schedule: "Sampling was conducted twice weekly in a given zone and scheduled within 2 d of the occurrence of quarter, full, last quarter, or new moon phases. One zone was sampled per week and zones were rotated monthly to ensure that each zone was sampled over all the lunar phases during the spawning season." (p. 193 (PDF p. 3))
- Grid selection: "Sampling grids were chosen by random selection with replacement to account for seasonal variability." (p. 193 (PDF p. 3))
- Effort per night: "5 grids were sampled per trip in Zone 1 and 6 grids were sampled per trip in Zones 2 and 3." (p. 195 (PDF p. 5))
- Stations per grid: "4 stations were sampled per grid and station locations were distributed as evenly as possible over 4 targeted bottom types: submerged aquatic vegetation, structure, channel, and nonchannel designations." (p. 195 (PDF p. 5))
- Hydrophone: "a mobile hydrophone (HTI, model 96-min, sensitivity−164 dBV µPa−1) was lowered 1 m into the water. After a 2 minute waiting period, all sciaenid courtship calls were identified and recorded." (p. 195 (PDF p. 5))
- Aggregation categories: "The estimated number of sand seatrout producing sound was categorized as (1) 1 to 2 individuals, (2) 3 to 5 individuals, or (3) an aggregation, the category used as the proxy for spawning." (p. 195 (PDF p. 5))
- Call identification: "Since male adult sand seatrout produce a species-specific call associated with reproduction (Locascio & Mann 2008), surveys using passive acoustic hydrophones can detect these calls and map spawning locations" (Introduction, p. 192 (PDF p. 2))
- Statistics: "the fixed effects of zone, year, and their interactions on aggregation percentage were tested by means of a generalized linear model (GLM) using a binomial distribution and maximum likelihood estimation (MLE) in PROC GLIMMIX of SAS 9.1 (SAS Institute). All tests were performed at α = 0.05." (p. 195 (PDF p. 5))
- Mapping software: "Locations of sand seatrout aggregations were mapped with Arc GIS 9.2" (p. 194 (PDF p. 5))
- Red tide sampling: "Water samples were collected approximately weekly from 4 fixed stations near the mouth of Tampa Bay (Fig. 1) for K. brevis cell concentrations." (Red tide data, p. 193 (PDF p. 3))
- Red tide data source: "Red tide data utilizing methods used to quantify Karenia brevis were obtained from the Harmful Algal Bloom (HAB) group of the FWC Fish and Wildlife Research Institute (FWRI)." (p. 193 (PDF p. 3))
- Cell enumeration: "Samples were examined and enumerated at 100× and 400× using an inverted microscope." (p. 193 (PDF p. 3))
- Red tide classes: "samples were ranked as being at background concentration (0 to 1000 cells l−1), bloom concentration (1001 to 100 000 cells l−1), or possible ichthyotoxic conditions (>100 000 cells l−1)." (p. 193 (PDF p. 3))
- Ethics protocol number: not stated in the paper.

## Quantitative claims

### Red tide timing

- "Red tide was not detected in Tampa Bay during the 2004 sand seatrout spawning period (April to September)." (Results, p. 195 (PDF p. 5))
- "Although Karenia brevis at ichthyotoxic concentrations was detected in 20% of samples (n = 80) from lower Tampa Bay in January and February, no other Tampa Bay samples (n = 52) collected from March through the end of 2004 contained K. brevis cells" (Results, p. 195 (PDF p. 5))
- "Background concentrations of Karenia brevis were first detected the second week of January at Stns 2 and 4 (Fig. 2). By mid-January, all 4 stations had K. brevis concentrations at bloom levels." (Results, p. 195 (PDF p. 5))
- "Bloom conditions persisted through February and increased to ichthyotoxic concentrations at Stn 3 on February 10 and at Stn 4 on February 28" (Results, p. 195 (PDF p. 5))
- "By June, 56% of samples from all 4 stations were at ichthyotoxic levels persisting throughout June and July (Table 1)" (Results, p. 196 (PDF p. 6))
- "Although ichthyotoxic levels of K. brevis were continuously detected in Zones 2 and 3 throughout the summer, these levels never extended into Zone 1." (Results, p. 196 (PDF p. 6))
- Initiation: "Initiated off the west-central Florida coast in early January 2005, the red tide bloom affected coastal waters through January 2006" (Introduction, p. 192 (PDF p. 2))

### Context values

- Background level: "Background levels (≤1000 cells l−1) of Karenia brevis are found year-round in the Gulf of Mexico (Tester & Steidinger 1997). Ichthyotoxic concentrations are considered to be 100 000 cells l−1" (Methods, p. 193 (PDF p. 3))
- Artificial reef effects reported by others: "fish species richness declining by > 50% and significant reductions in the abundances of most species (Dupont et al. 2010)" (Introduction, p. 192 (PDF p. 2))

## Methods worth reusing

- Spawning aggregation proxy: "an aggregation, the category used as the proxy for spawning" with 3 abundance categories per station (p. 195 (PDF p. 5)).
- Hydrophone protocol: "lowered 1 m into the water. After a 2 minute waiting period, all sciaenid courtship calls were identified and recorded." (p. 195 (PDF p. 5))
- Verification: "In the laboratory, field recordings were crossreferenced and verified against a standard sound reference library for other fish species of interest." (p. 195 (PDF p. 5))
- Station allocation: "4 stations were sampled per grid and station locations were distributed as evenly as possible over 4 targeted bottom types" and "If depth was constant throughout the grid, then the 4 sampling stations were distributed equidistant from one another throughout the grid." (p. 195 (PDF p. 5))
- Zone rotation: "zones were rotated monthly to ensure that each zone was sampled over all the lunar phases during the spawning season" (p. 193 (PDF p. 3)).
- Binomial GLM on percentage of stations with aggregations, fixed effects zone, year and interaction, PROC GLIMMIX in SAS 9.1, alpha 0.05 (p. 195 (PDF p. 5)).
- Before and after the red tide arrival within 2005 is mapped in Fig. 6, with April to June as before and July to September as after (Fig. 6 legend, p. 198 (PDF p. 8); read from the figure).
- Red tide categories for Karenia brevis: background 0 to 1000, bloom 1001 to 100 000, possible ichthyotoxic above 100 000 cells per litre (p. 193 (PDF p. 3)).

## Management statements

- "Incorporating spatially explicit environmental data into stock assessments will allow managers to account for perturbations, which conventional assessment methods were not designed to address (Rubec 1999)." (Discussion, p. 200 (PDF p. 10))
- "To further our understanding of the influence of environmental alterations on marine populations, we need to commit to studies with comprehensive spatial and temporal components in order to quantify losses to ecosystems." (Discussion, p. 200 (PDF p. 10))
- "Establishing baseline data, such as the annual juvenile abundance measures, can facilitate determinations of how an animal, community, or ecosystem is affected and if the effects are causal or coincidental" (Discussion, p. 200 (PDF p. 10))
- "Quantifiable data are requisite to gain true perspective on the biological consequences of a die-off event and potentially construct management actions to account for the stressed resources." (Introduction, p. 192 (PDF p. 2))
- "Incorporation of circulation models to approximate the spatial and temporal interactions between spawning sites, larval transport, and red tide could elucidate the relative contribution of larvae by zone" (Discussion, p. 200 (PDF p. 10))

## Stated limitations

- "it is difficult to determine with this methodology alone whether this decline was due to fish movement, cessation of spawning activity, or mortality." (Discussion, p. 197 (PDF p. 7))
- "As sampling in 2005 did not include Hillsborough Bay, it is unknown if the spawning population changed considerably in this area." (Discussion, p. 198 (PDF p. 8))
- "Since this study only involves 2 yr of data, there are limitations to inferring spawning trends without having data from the post-perturbation year (i.e. 2006)." (Discussion, p. 200 (PDF p. 10))
- "unequivocal data that would distinguish between significant and sustained fishery losses attributable to mortality from HAB toxicity and losses attributable to avoidance of an area have been difficult to obtain (Landsberg et al. 2009)" (Discussion, p. 199 (PDF p. 9))
- "cell counts are an approximation of potential lethality (Landsberg et al. 2009)" (Methods, p. 193 (PDF p. 3))
- Inference on cause: "K. brevis toxicity is likely the principal cause of the reduction in spawning." (Discussion, p. 199 (PDF p. 9)). The authors support this with spatial and temporal extent, severity, refugia limits and the juvenile series, and do not claim a direct measure of mortality.
- Only 2 years of hydrophone data and only one bloom year, so year is confounded with red tide exposure.
- Sampling was by zone rotation, so some zone and month cells are empty (Table 2).

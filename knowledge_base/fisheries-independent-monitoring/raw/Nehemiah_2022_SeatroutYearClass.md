# Nehemiah et al. 2022

Page locators follow the article's own "n of 11" page numbers, which match the PDF page numbers. They are written "p. n of 11 (PDF p. n)".

## Verbatim abstract

> "Environmental pressures can influence recruitment of estuarine fishes by impacting growth and survival of juveniles as well as adult habitat use and behavior. Identifying potential drivers of year-class strength is an important tool for assessing stock health and implementing management decisions. The goal of this study was to determine how environmental factors and adult stock influence trends in recruitment of Spotted Seatrout Cynoscion nebulosus in Cedar Key, Florida. Juvenile indices of abundance were generated using sampling data from the Florida Fish and Wildlife Conservation Commission's Fisheries-Independent Monitoring program. Young-of-the-year indices of abundance suggested that annual recruitment was variable in this system and declined throughout the majority of the 1996-2018 time series. Adult abundance and water temperature in the spawning season were the most significant variables in predicting Spotted Seatrout recruitment, and both had positive relationships with recruitment. Salinity and river discharge were not significant environmental variables in predicting year-class strength of Spotted Seatrout in this study. Our results indicated that recruitment was associated with interannual changes in water temperature and adult abundance, which could be used to explore how Spotted Seatrout recruitment dynamics could vary for stock assessments, for management decisions, and as a result of warming waters due to climate change." (Abstract, p. 1 of 11 (PDF p. 1))

## Study system and design

- Species and place: "determine how environmental factors and adult stock influence trends in recruitment of Spotted Seatrout Cynoscion nebulosus in Cedar Key, Florida." (Abstract, p. 1 of 11 (PDF p. 1))
- Region: "to evaluate whether environmental factors and spawning abundance were correlated with Spotted Seatrout recruitment in Cedar Key, on the west coast of Florida." (Introduction, p. 2-3 of 11 (PDF p. 2-3))
- Recruitment index period: "annual recruitment was variable in this system and declined throughout the majority of the 1996-2018 time series." (Abstract, p. 1 of 11 (PDF p. 1))
- Environmental data period: "Environmental data for the sampling area were gathered from the FIM sampling sites between 1997 and 2018." (Methods, p. 4 of 11 (PDF p. 4))
- Hypothesis 1: "years with high salinity and low river discharge would yield weak or below-average recruitment years as juvenile survivorship would be negatively impacted due to reduced habitat and prey availability" (Introduction, p. 3 of 11 (PDF p. 3))
- Hypothesis 2: "years with above average water temperature during the spawning season would positively influence year-class strength due to an extended spawning season and decreased stress from cold temperatures" (Introduction, p. 3 of 11 (PDF p. 3))
- Hypothesis 3: "adult abundance would have a significant positive relationship with juvenile recruitment due to increased population-level egg production." (Introduction, p. 3 of 11 (PDF p. 3))
- Spatial units: "The Cedar Key sampling area is divided into three zones based on the proximity to the Suwannee River (Figure 1)." (Methods, p. 3 of 11 (PDF p. 3))
- Zone B: "The northern region, closest to the Suwannee River is referred to as zone B" (Methods, p. 3 of 11 (PDF p. 3))
- Zone C: "the southern region, near Cedar Key, is referred to as zone C." (Methods, p. 3 of 11 (PDF p. 3))
- Zone F: "The sampling region within the Suwannee River itself is referred to as zone F." (Methods, p. 3 of 11 (PDF p. 3))
- Response variable for the regression: "Juvenile Spotted Seatrout year-class anomalies from the combined IOA estimates, using both zones B and C, were used as the response variable in this model." (Methods, p. 4 of 11 (PDF p. 4))
- Regression type: "A multiple linear regression was used to evaluate factors related to recruitment of Spotted Seatrout." (Methods, p. 4 of 11 (PDF p. 4))
- Variable selection: "Forward stepwise regression was used to determine which environmental variables were most critical for predicting year-class strength of Spotted Seatrout in Cedar Key." (Methods, p. 4 of 11 (PDF p. 4))
- Candidate predictors: "Possible explanatory variables in this model included adult Spotted Seatrout abundance, average water temperature during the spawning season (March to October), average salinity during peak juvenile abundance (June to November), and annual river discharge, including an interaction between river discharge and salinity." (Methods, p. 4 of 11 (PDF p. 4))
- Model selection criterion: "The best model was selected based on the Akaike information criterion (AIC) values." (Methods, p. 4 of 11 (PDF p. 4))
- Collinearity check: "Correlation analysis was conducted prior to model development to test for multicollinearity among explanatory variables." (Methods, p. 4 of 11 (PDF p. 4))
- Software and packages: not stated in the text. Ethics protocol: not applicable, no animals handled by the authors.

## FIM data use

### Program and design

- Program: "We used the Florida Fish and Wildlife Conservation Commission's FIM program sampling data that uses a multigear approach to target juvenile and subadult fish that are not yet vulnerable to fishing mortality." (Methods, p. 3 of 11 (PDF p. 3))
- Grid and strata: "Each sampled estuary is divided into grids of squares that are 1 nautical mile and stratified by habitat and gear type for each grid." (Methods, p. 3 of 11 (PDF p. 3))
- Frequency and start: "The FIM program has conducted monthly sampling at Cedar Key since 1996 (FIM 2017)." (Methods, p. 3 of 11 (PDF p. 3))
- Source for the zone map: "Map obtained from the 2016 Fisheries-Independent Monitoring Annual Report (FIM 2017)." (Figure 1 caption, p. 3 of 11 (PDF p. 3))

### Juvenile index

- Gear, size class and zones: "Indices of abundance for young-of-the-year Spotted Seatrout less than 100 mm SL were generated for Cedar Key using FIM catch data from 21.3-m center-bag seine nets in sampling zones B and C (Figure 1)." (Methods, p. 3-4 of 11 (PDF p. 3-4))
- Index definition: "The IOAs are standardized yearly mean catch per unit effort (CPUE) of juvenile Spotted Seatrout created using the delta method, which uses two generalized linear models to model occurrence and positive catches separately (Lo et al. 1992; Addis et al. 2018)." (Methods, p. 4 of 11 (PDF p. 4))
- Purpose of standardisation: "Catch rates were standardized to control for localized factors that affect the catch of juvenile Spotted Seatrout at each sampling site (e.g., bottom type and shore type)." (Methods, p. 4 of 11 (PDF p. 4))
- Positive-catch model: "The positive-catch model used a response variable of the log-transformed number of Spotted Seatrout in each set and assumed a log-normal error structure." (Methods, p. 4 of 11 (PDF p. 4))
- Zeros (occurrence model): "The binomial occurrence model had a response variable of either one or zero, representing presence or absence of young-of-the-year Spotted Seatrout." (Methods, p. 4 of 11 (PDF p. 4))
- Candidate standardisation variables: "Explanatory variables were selected through a forward stepwise regression, but possible variables included year, month, bottom vegetation, shoreline vegetation, bottom type, temperature, salinity, and sampling zone (Addis et al. 2018)." (Methods, p. 4 of 11 (PDF p. 4))
- Zone exclusion: "Zone F was excluded from this analysis due to low sampling size and limited gear types used in this zone (FIM 2017)." (Methods, p. 4 of 11 (PDF p. 4))
- Uncertainty: "Annual CPUE and variability were estimated using a Monte Carlo simulation with 10,000 iterations that used the annual least-squares mean estimated from the log-normal and binomial models." (Methods, p. 4 of 11 (PDF p. 4))
- Final index: "The final index was the product of the least-squares means estimates from the log-normal and binomial model, multiplied by their error structure (Addis et al. 2018)." (Methods, p. 4 of 11 (PDF p. 4))
- Zone-specific indices: "Estimated IOAs were also generated from the combined model to get recruitment indices for zone B and zone C." (Methods, p. 4 of 11 (PDF p. 4))
- Anomalies: "Recruitment anomalies were generated for each year by finding the difference between each yearly mean CPUE and the total average during the specified monitoring years." (Methods, p. 4 of 11 (PDF p. 4))
- Anomaly sign: "Positive and negative recruitment anomalies were represented by IOAs above and below the total mean CPUE values." (Methods, p. 4 of 11 (PDF p. 4))
- Baseline: "The mean CPUE across the monitoring years between 1996 and 2018 was taken based on the average IOA for each year." (Methods, p. 4 of 11 (PDF p. 4))
- Figure 2 units: the y-axis label reads "Number Per Sample" and the panels are "All Zones", "Zone B" and "Zone C" (Figure 2, p. 5 of 11 (PDF p. 5)).
- Figure 2 caption box definition: "the box dimensions represent the 2.5th to 7.5th percentile ranges, and whiskers show the 2.5th to 97.5th percentile ranges." (Figure 2 caption, p. 5 of 11 (PDF p. 5)). The Figure 5 caption gives "25th to 75th percentile ranges" for the same kind of box, so the Figure 2 text is likely a typographical slip.

(Methods, p. 4 of 11 (PDF p. 4))

- Adult anomalies: "Anomalies for adult abundance were created using similar methods as for the juvenile IOA anomalies previously discussed." (Methods, p. 4 of 11 (PDF p. 4))
- Figure 5 title: "Indices of abundance for adult Spotted Seatrout greater than 250 mm collected in Cedar Key, Florida, by the Fisheries-Independent Monitoring program from 1997 to 2018." (Figure 5 caption, p. 9 of 11 (PDF p. 9))

### Environmental covariates from FIM

- Seasonal windows: "For use in regression analysis, seasonal estimates of environmental variables were considered in order to test relationships during the spawning season or months of peak juvenile abundance." (Methods, p. 4 of 11 (PDF p. 4))
- Peak months definition: "Months of peak juvenile abundance were the months that FIM caught the most juvenile Spotted Seatrout annually." (Methods, p. 4 of 11 (PDF p. 4))
- Peak months in Cedar Key: "In Cedar Key, peak juvenile abundance was between June and November throughout the time series." (Methods, p. 4 of 11 (PDF p. 4))
- Salinity source: "Average monthly salinity estimates were obtained from water chemistry measurements collected during FIM sampling at 21.3-m-seine sites." (Methods, p. 4 of 11 (PDF p. 4))
- Salinity construction: "These estimates were the average salinity taken from the surface, the bottom, and that recorded at 1-m intervals between surface and bottom." (Methods, p. 4 of 11 (PDF p. 4))
- Salinity site filter: "Salinity estimates were only used from sites where juvenile Spotted Seatrout were present and captured in FIM sampling efforts." (Methods, p. 4 of 11 (PDF p. 4))
- Seasonal salinity: "Seasonal salinity was estimated by taking the average salinity during peak juvenile abundance." (Methods, p. 4 of 11 (PDF p. 4))
- Temperature source: "Water temperature was measured at all FIM 21.3-m seine-net sampling sites." (Methods, p. 4 of 11 (PDF p. 4))
- Temperature window: "Average water temperature between March and October was estimated and used as a predictor for recruitment anomalies." (Methods, p. 4 of 11 (PDF p. 4))
- Window rationale: "Previous studies in Florida observed that Spotted Seatrout spawning seasons may persist from March to October (Brown-Peterson et al. 2002). This range was selected as an indicator of temperature during the spawning season." (Methods, p. 4 of 11 (PDF p. 4))
- Discharge source (not FIM): "River discharge was taken from the U.S. Geological Survey Suwannee River gauge at Wilcox, Florida." (Methods, p. 4 of 11 (PDF p. 4))
- Discharge record: "This gauge has recorded daily real-time discharge of the Suwannee River since 1986." (Methods, p. 4 of 11 (PDF p. 4))
- Discharge metric: "Annual discharge estimates were downloaded to find patterns between river discharge and annual recruitment in Cedar Key." (Methods, p. 4 of 11 (PDF p. 4))

### Assumptions and limits

- Index assumption: "this study assumes that the IOAs generated from FIM sampling data are an appropriate measurement of year-class strength for this species." (Discussion, p. 9 of 11 (PDF p. 9))
- Sampling and mortality assumption: "This metric assumes that juvenile Spotted Seatrout are adequately sampled in this estuary and that major mortality events occur before fish reach 100 mm. These assumptions may not be appropriate for this population." (Discussion, p. 9 of 11 (PDF p. 9))
- Zone F exclusion reason: see Zone exclusion above (low sampling size and limited gear types).

## Quantitative claims

### Headline results

- Model fit: "this model explained nearly 70% of the observed variation of annual recruitment in Cedar Key (adjusted R2 = 0.699)." (Results, p. 5 of 11 (PDF p. 5))
- Selected variables: "Forward stepwise regression selected annual adult population size and water temperature in the spawning season as significant explanatory variables to predict recruitment (Table 1)." (Results, p. 5 of 11 (PDF p. 5))
- Direction: "Adult abundance and water temperature had a positive relationship with Spotted Seatrout year-class." (Results, p. 5 of 11 (PDF p. 5))
- Fit to observations: "Predicted recruitment anomalies from the model demonstrated similar annual trends when compared with the observed recruitment anomalies (Figure 3)." (Results, p. 5-6 of 11 (PDF p. 5-6))
- Overall index trend: "In general, recruitment declined from 1998 to 2013, with the weakest year-class observed in 2013." (Results, p. 5 of 11 (PDF p. 5))

### Year-class timeline

- Strong years: "The IOAs indicated strong recruitment years in 1997 and 1998." (Results, p. 5 of 11 (PDF p. 5))
- Recent years: "Recruitment appeared to increase from 2013 to 2016, while the most recent years 2017 and 2018 were average year-classes (Figure 2)." (Results, p. 5 of 11 (PDF p. 5))
- Zone B trend: "Zone B IOAs showed a decreasing trend from 1997 to 2013." (Results, p. 5 of 11 (PDF p. 5))
- Zone B recent years: "While the 2016 year-class appeared to be a relatively strong year-class, 2017 and 2018 were both weak year-classes." (Results, p. 5 of 11 (PDF p. 5))
- Zone C: "Zone C IOAs indicated fairly stable recruitment trends across the time series, with poor year-classes observed in 2011 and 2013 and an increasing trend in recruitment from 2013 to 2018 (Figure 2)." (Results, p. 5 of 11 (PDF p. 5))
- Spatial pattern: "Estimated IOAs for zones B and C suggested variable trends in recruitment based on proximity to the Suwannee River." (Results, p. 5 of 11 (PDF p. 5))

### Standardisation terms

- Positive-catch model: "Significant (P-value < 0.05) explanatory variables used to standardize juvenile Spotted Seatrout CPUE in the combined model included year, zone, bottom vegetation, and a year × zone interaction term in the positive-catch model." (Results, p. 5 of 11 (PDF p. 5))
- Binomial model: "Significant explanatory variables in the binomial model included year, zone, month, bottom vegetation, shore type, bottom type, and a zone × year interaction term." (Results, p. 5 of 11 (PDF p. 5))

### Correlations

- Highest correlation: "The highest correlation observed was between annual river discharge and salinity during peak juvenile abundance (r = -0.565); the remaining Pearson correlation coefficients were all less than 0.5." (Results, p. 5 of 11 (PDF p. 5))
- Overall: "Correlation analysis did not suggest that these tested explanatory variables had strong correlation with each other." (Results, p. 5 of 11 (PDF p. 5))

## Methods worth reusing

- Delta index: "The IOAs are standardized yearly mean catch per unit effort (CPUE) of juvenile Spotted Seatrout created using the delta method, which uses two generalized linear models to model occurrence and positive catches separately (Lo et al. 1992; Addis et al. 2018)." (Methods, p. 4 of 11 (PDF p. 4))
- Variance propagation: "Annual CPUE and variability were estimated using a Monte Carlo simulation with 10,000 iterations that used the annual least-squares mean estimated from the log-normal and binomial models." (Methods, p. 4 of 11 (PDF p. 4))
- Juvenile definition: "young-of-the-year Spotted Seatrout less than 100 mm SL" from "21.3-m center-bag seine nets in sampling zones B and C". Adult definition: "greater than 250 mm SL captured in FIM's 183-m seine net". (Methods, p. 3-4 of 11 (PDF p. 3-4))
- Seasonal covariate windows: spawning-season temperature March to October; salinity June to November (peak juvenile abundance); discharge annual (Methods, p. 4 of 11 (PDF p. 4))
- Stepwise recruitment model: "Forward stepwise regression was used to determine which environmental variables were most critical for predicting year-class strength of Spotted Seatrout in Cedar Key." (Methods, p. 4 of 11 (PDF p. 4))
- Anomaly construction: "Recruitment anomalies were generated for each year by finding the difference between each yearly mean CPUE and the total average during the specified monitoring years." (Methods, p. 4 of 11 (PDF p. 4))
- Time-series alignment: the juvenile index starts in 1996 and the environmental and adult series start in 1997 (Methods, p. 3-4 of 11). The regression therefore uses 1997 to 2018 at most; the paper does not state the number of years in the regression.

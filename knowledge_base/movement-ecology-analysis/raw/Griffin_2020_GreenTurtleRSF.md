# Griffin et al. 2020

## Verbatim abstract

> "Background: A better understanding of sea turtle spatial ecology is critical for the continued conservation of imperiled sea turtles and their habitats. For resource managers to develop the most effective conservation strategies, it is especially important to examine how turtles use and select for habitats within their developmental foraging grounds. Here, we examine the space use and relative habitat selection of immature green turtles (Chelonia mydas) using acoustic telemetry within the marine protected area, Buck Island Reef National Monument (BIRNM), St. Croix, United States Virgin Islands. Results: Space use by turtles was concentrated on the southern side of Buck Island, but also extended to the northeast and northwest areas of the island, as indicated by minimum convex polygons (MCPs) and 99%, 95%, and 50% kernel density estimations (KDEs). On average space use for all categories was < 3 km2 with mean KDE area overlap ranging from 41.9 to 67.7%. Cumulative monthly MCPs and their proportions to full MCPs began to stabilize 3 to 6 detection months after release, respectively. Resource selection functions (RSFs) were implemented using a generalized linear mixed effects model with turtle ID as the random effect. After model selection, the accuracy of the top model was 77.3% and showed relative habitat selection values were highest at shallow depths, for areas in close proximity to seagrass, and in reef zones for both day and night, and within lagoon zones at night. The top model was also extended to predict across BIRNM at both day and night. Conclusion: More traditional acoustic telemetry analyses in combination with RSFs provide novel insights into animal space use and relative resource selection. Here, we demonstrated immature green turtles within the BIRNM have small, specific home ranges and core use areas with temporally varying relative selection strengths across habitat types. We conclude the BIRNM marine protected area is providing sufficient protection for immature green turtles, however, habitat protection could be focused in both areas of high space use and in locations where high relative selection values were determined. Ultimately, the methodologies and results presented here may help to design strategies to expand habitat protection for immature green turtles across their greater distribution." (Abstract, p. 1 (PDF p. 1))

## Study system and design

- Species: "immature green turtles (Chelonia mydas)" (Abstract, p. 1 (PDF p. 1)).
- Site: "Buck Island Reef National Monument (BIRNM), St. Croix, United States Virgin Islands" (Abstract, p. 1 (PDF p. 1)). No coordinates are given in the text.
- Reserve: "BIRNM, managed by the United States National Park Service, was established in 1961 and expanded in 2001. This protected area, located on the northeastern shelf of St. Croix, is a 73.4-km2 no-take marine reserve with small restricted anchoring zone" (Methods, Study site, p. 8 (PDF p. 8)).
- Island: "Uninhabited, Buck Island (0.7 km2) is in the center of BIRNM and 2.5 km northeast of St. Croix, USVI." (Methods, Study site, p. 8 (PDF p. 8)).
- Habitat: "This reef system encompasses a 50- to 150-m-wide lagoon." and "Sea grass beds comprised Thalassia sp., Syringodium sp., Halophila sp." (Methods, Study site, p. 8 (PDF p. 8)).
- Array: "A passive acoustic receiver array, designed to study multiple species [53, 61, 65–68], at BIRNM was expanded from six to 141 Vemco VR2W receivers (Vemco Amirix Systems, NS, Canada) between 2011 and 2018." (Methods, Study site, p. 8 (PDF p. 8)).
- Receiver depth and servicing: "the majority being deployed in < 15 m. Receiver data downloads and maintenance were performed twice a year." (Methods, Study site, p. 8 (PDF p. 8)).
- Capture: "green turtles were captured by hand while freediving or by “turtle jumping” rodeo method" (Methods, Turtle capture and tagging, p. 8 (PDF p. 8)).
- Morphometrics taken: "curved (CCL) and straight carapace nuchal (SCLn) and total (SCLt) lengths, width, height, biological samples" (Methods, Turtle capture and tagging, p. 8 (PDF p. 8)).
- Tag models: "V16-4L (16 × 88 mm, 24 g in air, 69 kHz, 152 dB, with 30–90 s delay interval, Vemco Amirix Systems, NS, Canada) or V13-1L (13 × 36 mm, 11 g in air, 60–84 kHz, 147 dB, with 30–90 s delay interval, Vemco Amirix Systems, NS, Canada)" (Methods, Turtle capture and tagging, p. 9 (PDF p. 9)).
- Attachment: "attached to individual turtles via a coated wire secured through the right or left marginal carapace scute and by using half a tube of West Marine epoxy [48]" (Methods, Turtle capture and tagging, p. 9 (PDF p. 9)).
- Years and sample: "58 immature green turtles within the BIRNM using 68 transmitters (due to retagging) deployed across 2012 (n = 38) and 2013 (n = 30)" (Results, p. 3 (PDF p. 3)).
- Space-use sample: "52 immature green turtles (with 58 transmitters due to retagging) across 2012, 2013, and 2014" (Results, p. 3 (PDF p. 3)).
- Ethics: "Fieldwork was permitted under NMFS permits 16146 and 20315, issued to K. Hart, National Park IACUC USGS-SESC2014-02 and USGS IACUC WARC\GNV 2017-04. Additional permits issued to K. Hart BUIS-2011-SCI-0012; BUIS-2014SCI-0009; BUIS-2016-SCI-0009." (Ethics approval, p. 11 (PDF p. 11)).
- Software: "analyzed it the R statistical environment (R Development Core Team 2018)" (Methods, Data filtering and organization, p. 9 (PDF p. 9)); packages glatos, VTRACK, adehabitatHR, raster, sp, maptools, glmmTMB, MuMIn, sjPlot (Methods, pp. 9 to 10 (PDF pp. 9 to 10)).
- Statistical approach: "we applied RSFs using GLMMs with a binomial distribution similar to the approach presented by Selby et al. [58]" (Methods, RSFs and GLMMs, p. 9 (PDF p. 9)).
- Funding: "We acknowledge the USGS Natural Resource Protection Program (NRPP) for funding." (Funding, p. 11 (PDF p. 11)).
- Dates: "Received: 29 February 2020 Accepted: 5 June 2020" (p. 11 (PDF p. 11)).

## Quantitative claims

### Sample sizes

- 58 turtles, 68 transmitters, 38 in 2012, 30 in 2013: "we examined the detection logs of 58 immature green turtles within the BIRNM using 68 transmitters (due to retagging) deployed across 2012 (n = 38) and 2013 (n = 30) (Additional file 1: Figs. S1, S2, Table S1)." (Results, p. 3 (PDF p. 3)).
- Turtle size: "Individual size of tagged turtles ranged from 33.8 to 65.5 cm (straight line carapace nuchal [SCLn]; 46.9 ± 7.0 cm)." (Results, p. 3 (PDF p. 3)).
- 10 logs dropped, 97,065 COAs, 52 turtles, 58 transmitters: "After removing detection logs with less than 100 centers of activity (COAs) (n = 10), we used a total of 97,065 centers of activity to examine the space use of 52 immature green turtles (with 58 transmitters due to retagging) across 2012, 2013, and 2014." (Results, p. 3 (PDF p. 3)).
- Four transmitters active at 10 months: "Tracking duration was limited, with only four transmitters remaining active 10 detection months after release." (Results, p. 3 (PDF p. 3)).

### Tracking duration

- Days at liberty: "Days at liberty (i.e., tracking duration) ranged from 1 to 505 days (146.3 ± 100.1 days)" (Results, p. 3 (PDF p. 3)).
- Residency: "residency values (i.e., detection days/days at liberty) ranged from 0.6 to 1 (0.8 ± 0.3)." (Results, p. 3 (PDF p. 3)).

### Space use

- Full MCP: "Full minimum convex polygon (MCP) values for detection logs ranged from 0.0 to 4.2 km2 (0.9 ± 0.9 km2)." (Results, p. 3 (PDF p. 3)).
- Stabilisation: "cumulative monthly MCP values began to stabilize around six detection months and proportions to their full MCPs appeared to stabilize around three detection months after release." (Results, p. 3 (PDF p. 3)).
- 99% KDE: "Constructed 99% kernel density estimates (KDEs) ranged from 1.2 to 5.9 km2 (2.3 ± 0.8 km2) with a mean overlap of 67.7% (± 19.3%)" (Results, p. 3 (PDF p. 3)).
- 95% KDE: "95% KDEs range from 0.7 to 3.7 km2 (1.4 ± 0.5 km2) with a mean overlap of 64.5% (± 21.25%)" (Results, p. 3 (PDF p. 3)).
- 50% KDE: "50% KDEs ranged from 0.1 to 0.6 km2 (0.3 ± 0.0 km2) with a mean overlap of 41.9% (± 33.6%) (Fig. 2)." (Results, p. 3 (PDF p. 3)).
- Overall: "On average space use for all categories was < 3 km2 with mean KDE area overlap ranging from 41.9 to 67.7%." (Abstract, p. 1 (PDF p. 1)).
- Location: "While 95% and 50% KDEs were largely located on the southern side of Buck Island, some individual 95% KDEs extended around to the northeast and northwest areas of the island as well (Fig. 3)." (Results, p. 3 (PDF p. 3)).

### Model results

- Collinearity: "Variance inflation factors revealed reef type and zone type were highly correlated and, thus, we decided to remove reef type from the model." (Results, p. 3 (PDF p. 3)).
- Top model: "The top RSF generalized linear mixed model (GLMM) (using a random 60% subset of the data) was the full model, as indicated via model selection (Additional file 1: Table S2)." (Results, p. 3 (PDF p. 3)).
- Year random effect: "summary outputs for the final model indicated the random effect, year, had a variance component equal to zero, thus was removed in further analysis." (Results, p. 3 (PDF p. 3)).
- Accuracy: "overall model accuracy was 77.3% with predictions of 1 s at 72.2%." (Results, p. 3 (PDF p. 3)).
- Background accuracy: "our model predicted 0 s at 85.2% accuracy." (Results, p. 3 (PDF p. 3)).
- Accuracy by level: "Model performance across the three categorical variables (diel period, and zone type) and their levels ranged from 71.7 to 100% (77.1 ± 11.3, Additional file 1: Table S3)." (Results, p. 3 (PDF p. 3)).

### Habitat selection

- Seagrass: "Relative selection by turtles was high in areas 200 m or closer to seagrass in both day and night (Fig. 4a)." (Results, p. 3 (PDF p. 3)).
- Depth: "Shallower depths (less than 10 m) were found to have higher relative selection probabilities both day and night, however, there were higher relative selection probabilities in the day (Fig. 4a)." (Results, p. 3 (PDF p. 3)).
- Zone: "For zone type, turtles had the three highest relative selection probabilities within reef zones at day, reef zones at night, and within lagoon zones at night (Fig. 4a)." (Results, p. 3 (PDF p. 3)).
- Prediction: "the greatest relative selection probabilities for immature green turtles were located within shallow depths near Buck Island and generally decreased farther away from the island at deeper depths (Fig. 5)." (Results, p. 3 (PDF p. 3)).
- Selection at depths > 10 m (Discussion value): "tiger sharks within BIRNM were more likely to inhabit depths > 10 m, at which point, relative habitat selection for turtles was very low (< 0.25)." (Discussion, p. 6 (PDF p. 6)).
- Tracking needed: "our MCP metrics suggested 3 to 6 months of tracking for turtles may provide sufficient amount of data for inferences." (Discussion, pp. 3 to 5 (PDF pp. 3 to 5)).

### Cited comparison values

- Hawaii and Florida home ranges (Brill et al. 1995 [54]; Makowski et al. 2006 [42]): "the estimated home ranges for immature green turtles in Hawaii being < 3 km2 [54] and in Florida were 3 km2 [42]." (Discussion, p. 6 (PDF p. 6)).
- Core use areas (Makowski et al. 2006 [42]; Chambault et al. 2020 [55]): "similar to that reported by Makowski et al. [42] (0.5 ± 0.4 km2) and Chambault et al. [55] (0.2 ± 0.3 km2)." (Discussion, p. 6 (PDF p. 6)).
- Tiger shark depth (Casselberry et al. 2020 [53]): "acoustically tagged tiger sharks within BIRNM were more likely to inhabit depths > 10 m" (Discussion, p. 6 (PDF p. 6)).
- Satellite tagging and MPAs (Scott et al. 2012 [34]; Hart et al. 2013 [35]): "35% of satellite tagged green turtles aggregated within MPAs, while Hart et al. [35] highlighted 82% of satellite tagged green turtles were within local and regional MPAs in Florida." (Background, p. 2 (PDF p. 2)).
- Culebra distance (Griffin et al. 2019 [47]): "the neighboring island, Culebra Island, Puerto Rico (< 100 km), also remained in welldefined areas [47]." (Discussion, pp. 6 to 7 (PDF pp. 6 to 7)).

## Methods worth reusing

- Background filter: "Data were first filtered for background detections using the background_detections function (time threshold set at 2700 s) in the glatos package" (Methods, Data filtering and organization, p. 9 (PDF p. 9)).
- Speed and interval filter: "detections of an individual were removed if the rate of movement exceeded 10 m/s or had a time difference of less than 30 s from its last detection." (Methods, p. 9 (PDF p. 9)).
- Tag-loss cut-off: "Using the detection_events function, from the glatos package, we identified the last “event” in each turtle’s detection log, in this case, a new event was defined as when two consecutive detections are logged across two different receivers. We also modified this function to incorporate distance, for example, we identified events when turtles exceeded 400 m." (Methods, p. 9 (PDF p. 9)).
- Centres of activity: "transformed it into COAs using the mean position algorithm with 60-min bins [70]. COAs were generated with the VTRACK R package [71]" (Methods, p. 9 (PDF p. 9)).
- Cumulative MCPs: "Calculated MCPs for subsequent detection months would be cumulatively added to the first MCP calculated, thus cumulative monthly MCPs." and "the last cumulative monthly MCP in an individual’s detection log will always be equal to the observed full MCP." (Methods, p. 9 (PDF p. 9)).
- Detection months: "detection months after release refers to the subsequent months an individual was detected excluding any monthly gaps in detection history." (Methods, p. 9 (PDF p. 9)).
- Minimum data threshold: "following Selby et al. [58], we eliminated individual transmitter datasets with less than 100 COAs." (Methods, p. 9 (PDF p. 9)).
- Kernel settings: "All KUDs and KDEs were derived using the adehabitatHR package [74] and with a 200 m smoothing parameter." (Methods, p. 9 (PDF p. 9)).
- Overlap definition: "percent overlap is defined by the proportion of individual is KDE that is overlapped by individual js KDE [75]." (Methods, p. 9 (PDF p. 9)).
- Response coding: "The response variable included turtle presence locations (COAs), coded as 1 s, and random background points, coded as 0 s." (Methods, RSFs and GLMMs, p. 9 (PDF p. 9)).
- Background points: "Random background points were randomly placed at an equal number of random locations per individual’s COAs within the available observable area matching the same number of observed diel periods (day vs. night) and observed points per year." (Methods, p. 10 (PDF p. 10)).
- Availability buffer: "we generated a 400-m buffer, at any given receiver at a given year (i.e., 2012, 2013, 2014, Additional file 1: Fig. S3). Following Selby et al. [58], we chose a 400-m buffer based on both previous range-testing of the BIRNM array [82] and to retain higher numbers of calculated COAs." (Methods, p. 10 (PDF p. 10)).
- Raster grid: "The calculated COAs (presences) and random background points were collapsed into 200 × 200 m raster cells, along with habitat and depth." (Methods, p. 10 (PDF p. 10)).
- Habitat variables: "distance to seagrass (seagrass defined as > 50% coverage, Fig. 4b), reef type (no reef, patch reef, continuous reef, Additional file 1: Fig. S4), and zone type (lagoon, bank/shelf, reef, other, Fig. 4b)." (Methods, p. 10 (PDF p. 10)).
- Depth gaps: "cells with no data available were replaced with the mean values of adjacent cells." (Methods, p. 10 (PDF p. 10)).
- Diel assignment: "diel period (day and night) were assigned to all points using civil dawn and civil dusk times within the maptools package [85]." (Methods, p. 10 (PDF p. 10)).
- Train and holdout split: "GLMMs were implemented using random subsets of 60% of the dataset. The remaining 40% of the dataset (holdout dataset) was later used to test model accuracy." (Methods, p. 10 (PDF p. 10)).
- Full model: "The full model included diel and depth as an interaction term, diel and distance to seagrass as an interaction term, diel and zone type as an interaction term, and individual and year as random effects. All continuous variables were standardized (z-standardization)." (Methods, p. 10 (PDF p. 10)).
- Retagged individuals: "Datasets from individuals that had multiple transmitters attached (due to tag loss and subsequent recapture and retagging) were grouped together, respectively." (Methods, p. 10 (PDF p. 10)).
- Fitting and selection: "We fit the full model using the glmmTMB package [86] and used the MuMIn package [87] to run all possible subsets, i.e., combination of coefficients." and "We then used the Akaike information criteria (AIC) to select the top model." (Methods, p. 10 (PDF p. 10)).
- Accuracy definition: "Model accuracy was defined as the top model’s ability to correctly predict the probability of 1 s and 0 s using the 40% data subset." (Methods, p. 10 (PDF p. 10)).
- Marginal effects: "Marginal effects plots were generated for each conditional term in the final model using the sjPlot package [88]." and "the continuous variables, depth and distance to seagrass, were held at their means and all other factor variables were held constant at their proportional level rather than at their reference levels." (Methods, p. 10 (PDF p. 10)).
- Spatial prediction: "using the aggregated raster layers, we used the top model to predict and plot the relative selection probabilities across the entire study area for both day and night." (Methods, p. 10 (PDF p. 10)).

## Management statements

- "We conclude the BIRNM marine protected area is providing sufficient protection for immature green turtles, however, habitat protection could be focused in both areas of high space use and in locations where high relative selection values were determined." (Abstract, p. 1 (PDF p. 1)).
- "Thus, detailed habitat protection (e.g., no anchor, motor and idle only zones, invasive seagrass control) could be focused in areas of space use and in locations with high relative selection probabilities, as indicated by the RSF model." (Discussion, p. 8 (PDF p. 8)).
- "Considering immature green turtles use and have the highest predicted relative selection values near Buck Island, the BIRNM MPA is providing sufficient protection for developmental grounds." (Discussion, p. 7 (PDF p. 7)).
- "it is critical that acoustic arrays are designed to initially capture the representative habitats to reduce biases." (Discussion, p. 8 (PDF p. 8)).
- "with this application, resource managers within and outside MPAs can refocus enforcement, habitat protection and restoration in areas of need to reach conservation endpoints." (Conclusion, p. 8 (PDF p. 8)).

## Stated limitations

- "Tracking duration was limited, with only four transmitters remaining active 10 detection months after release. This is likely an effect of transmitter retention due to tear-outs from attachment holes failing and/or tags moving towards the edge of their scutes from growth [48] (Fig. 1)." (Results, p. 3 (PDF p. 3)).
- "space use did not perfectly match the predicted relative selection probabilities that were observed across a much broader spatial extent. This may be due to tagging effort being generally located south of the island where green turtle abundances were highest" (Discussion, p. 7 (PDF p. 7)).
- "the RSF model could have been improved with additional covariates that capture additional environmental characteristics (e.g., fetch, wave energy, seagrass total area), ecological processes (e.g., density dependence, predator landscape metrics, [57]), and green turtle foraging strategies." (Discussion, p. 7 (PDF p. 7)).
- "RSFs are inherently phenomenological and not mechanistic, meaning while space use is an interaction between movement and habitat selection, these RSF predictions are solely focused on observed habitat selection patterns and do not take into account movement processes (e.g., home ranging behavior)." (Discussion, p. 8 (PDF p. 8)).
- "Important animal space use-habitat relationships may be lost if cell sizes are set too large since the aggregation process selects the dominant habitat type within a cell for classification." (Discussion, p. 8 (PDF p. 8)).
- "these analyses focus on relative selection rather than probability of occurrence, meaning that there may be additional variables to explore to improve the accuracy of interpolated space use across an entire area other than RSFs (e.g., integrated step selection functions)." (Discussion, p. 8 (PDF p. 8)).
- "Here, we also note V16-4Ls are more powerful than V13-1Ls and, theoretically, are able to be detected at greater distances." (Methods, p. 9 (PDF p. 9)).

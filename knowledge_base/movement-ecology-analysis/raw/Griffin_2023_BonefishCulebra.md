# Griffin et al. 2023

## Verbatim abstract

> "Bonefish (Albula spp.) have ecological, economic, and cultural importance throughout their tropical and subtropical range. These fish reside primarily in shallow, nearshore habitats, and their movement patterns are largely dominated by tidal flows, thermal regime, and seasonal spawning migrations. Previous studies of their spatial ecology show that bonefish exhibit moderate site fidelity to specific tidal creeks and flats; however, to date, limited research has looked at movement patterns of bonefish that reside in small fringing reef flats, such as those found associated with some islands in the Caribbean. This study used fixed station acoustic telemetry to quantify the movement patterns of bonefish inhabiting small fringing reef flats in the nearshore waters of Culebra, Puerto Rico, for nearly 3 years. Bonefish inhabiting these flats exhibited high site fidelity and small home ranges, with limited movements to flats that were no further than 3 km away. Network analyses revealed distinct groups of bonefish that were associated with the specific reef flats where they were tagged. This high site fidelity has considerable implications for the risk of disturbance to bonefish inhabiting reef flats. These small, isolated groups of fish are likely vulnerable to localized impacts such as habitat degradation or harvest and highly dependent on these specific locations." (Abstract, p. 433 (PDF p. 1))

## Study system and design

- Species: "bonefish (Albula vulpes)" (Title, p. 433 (PDF p. 1)).
- Site and coordinates: "The study took place from July 2012 to June 2015 on Culebra, Puerto Rico (18.31578°N, –65.28763°W)." (Methods, Study sites, p. 435 (PDF p. 3))
- Island size: "Culebra is a relatively small island (28 km2)" (Methods, Study sites, p. 435 (PDF p. 3)).
- Habitat depths: "shallow reef flats (< 2 m deep) separated by deeper channels (8–10 m deep)" (Methods, Study sites, p. 435 (PDF p. 3)).
- Focal flats: "we concentrated our efforts on two main flats and their adjacent waters. We heretofore refer to these flats only in terms of their general localities, Dakity or Manglar Bay (Fig. 1a)." (Methods, Study sites, p. 435 (PDF p. 3))
- Benthic habitats: "sand and coral rubble flats, seagrass beds (Thalassia testudinum, Syringodium filiforme, Halodule wrightii), macroalgae (e.g., Halimeda spp., Penicillus spp.), sponges, hard bottom, and small patches of live coral." (Methods, Study sites, p. 435 (PDF p. 3))
- Receivers: "The telemetry array included 59 fixed acoustic receivers (VR2W receivers, Innovasea, Amirix Inc., Bedford, NS, Canada)" (Methods, Acoustic tracking system, p. 435 (PDF p. 3)).
- Positioning sub-array: "deployed as part of a Vemco Positioning Systems (VPS) sub-array within Manglar Bay (n = 25)" (Methods, Acoustic tracking system, p. 435 (PDF p. 3)).
- Receiver aggregation: "VPS receivers that were in the lagoon were aggregated (n = 17) as a single node and those predominantly around the reef (n = 8) were aggregated together." (Methods, Acoustic tracking system, p. 435 (PDF p. 3))
- Final node count and array area: "position data were available for 36 distinct locations (Fig. 1a) with an approximate area (including land) of 40 km2." (Methods, Acoustic tracking system, p. 435 (PDF p. 3))
- Receiver mooring: "secured to a 60-cm piece of rebar anchored into a cement base (50 × 30 × 10 cm, approx. 30 kg)" (Methods, Acoustic tracking system, p. 435 (PDF p. 3)).
- Receiver orientation: "situated vertically when depths were greater than 1 m (at low tide), and oriented either horizontally or 5–10° above horizontal when the water depth at low tide was less than 1 m" (Methods, Acoustic tracking system, p. 435 (PDF p. 3)).
- Detection range, as assessed for the same array in Griffin et al. (2019a): "our shallow water flats receivers had 50% detection efficiency at a radius of 80 m." (Methods, Acoustic tracking system, p. 435 (PDF p. 3))
- Tagging periods: "Tags were deployed over two time intervals, occurring between 25 June and 10 Aug 2012 and between 23 April and 25 May 2013." (Methods, Tagging, p. 435 (PDF p. 3))
- Animals tagged: "A total of 43 bonefish (n = 20, Dakity; n = 23, Manglar Bay, Fig. 1b) were surgically implanted" (Methods, Tagging, p. 435 (PDF p. 3)).
- Coded tag model: "V13-1L coded acoustic transmitter tags (13 mm diameter, 36 mm long, 6.0 g in air, min and max delay times 45 to 135 s, 880-day battery life, Innovasea, Amirix Inc., Bedford, NS, Canada)" (Methods, Tagging, p. 435 (PDF p. 3)).
- Accelerometer tag model: "V13AP accelerometer tags (12.2 g, 45–135 s transmission delay, ± 3.43 g acceleration range, 323-day battery life, 5 Hz sampling frequency, Innovasea, Amirix Inc., Bedford, NS, Canada)" (Methods, Tagging, p. 435 (PDF p. 3)).
- Capture gear: "fly-fishing gear (8–9 weight rods, 6 kg break strength leaders with no. 4–8 flies) or spinning gear (medium-heavy strength rods, 6 kg break strength monofilament line with 1/0 circle hooks with small live crabs ~ 2 cm width)" (Methods, Tagging, p. 435 to 436 (PDF p. 3 to 4)).
- Size cutoff: "only bonefish that were greater than or equal to 410 mm were tagged." (Methods, Tagging, p. 436 (PDF p. 4))
- Anaesthesia: "anesthetized with MS-222 (approx. 100 ppm) in a cooler (45 L)" and "a maintenance dose of MS 222 (approx. 50 ppm) in fresh seawater" (Methods, Tagging, p. 436 to 437 (PDF p. 4 to 5)).
- Surgery: "a small incision (2–3 cm)", "closed with 2–4 interrupted sutures (Ethicon 3–0 PDS II, monofilament absorbable suture material, Johnson and Johnson, New Jersey)", "The entire surgical procedure took less than 5 min" (Methods, Tagging, p. 437 (PDF p. 5)).
- Recovery: "Fish were then held for up to 30 min in a floating net pen (1.2L × 1.2 W × 1.2H m) in situ prior to release" (Methods, Tagging, p. 437 (PDF p. 5)).
- Ethics protocol: "approved under the University of Massachusetts Amherst IACUC protocol #2010–004." (Methods, Tagging, p. 437 (PDF p. 5)); the Declarations give "UMass Amherst IACUC (protocol #2010–0004)" (Declarations, p. 444 (PDF p. 12)). The two numbers differ by one zero in the source.
- Software: "All statistical analyses were conducted using R 3.6.2 (R Core Team 2019) and R studio 1.4.953 (RStudio Team 2020) and, unless otherwise indicated, means are presented with one standard deviation." (Methods, Data analyses, p. 437 (PDF p. 5))
- Packages named: VTrack (Campbell et al. 2012), DHARMa (Hartig 2017), glmmTMB (Brooks et al. 2017), performance (Lüdecke et al. 2019), igraph (Csardi and Nepusz 2006) (Methods, Data analyses, p. 437 to 438 (PDF p. 5 to 6)).
- Statistical tests: Student's t-test for size between tagging areas; linear regressions of residency, dispersal distance and MCP on fish size; Poisson generalized linear mixed model for trips versus lunar phase; Fast-Greedy community detection with Wilcoxon rank-sum confirmation (Methods, Data analyses, p. 437 to 438 (PDF p. 5 to 6)).

## Quantitative claims

### Sample sizes

- 43 fish, mean size: "A total of 43 bonefish (522 ± 56 mm) were tagged for this study" (Results, p. 438 (PDF p. 6)).
- Size by area: "bonefish tagged at Manglar Bay (540 ± 57 mm) were significantly larger than those tagged at Dakity (501 ± 48 mm) (Supplementary Information Table S1)." (Results, p. 438 (PDF p. 6))
- Fish with MCP estimates: "For tagged individuals with enough locations for MCP calculations (n = 32)" (Results, p. 438 (PDF p. 6)).

### Tracking duration

- All fish: "Across all bonefish, mean tracking duration was 412 days (± 237 days)" (Results, p. 438 (PDF p. 6)).
- 880-day tags: "for fish tagged with transmitters that had 880-day battery lives, mean tracking duration was 457 days (± 246 days)" (Results, p. 438 (PDF p. 6)).
- 323-day tags: "for fish tagged with transmitters that had 323-day battery lives, mean tracking duration was 243 days (± 61 days)." (Results, p. 438 (PDF p. 6))

### Residency and dispersal

- Residency: "Overall residency was 0.78 (± 0.25)" (Results, p. 438 (PDF p. 6)).
- Dispersal: "overall dispersal distances from respective tagging locations ranged from 0 to 3.52 km (1.42 ± 1.08 km) (Supplementary Information Table S1)." (Results, p. 438 (PDF p. 6))

### Home range

- MCP range and mean: "MCP estimates ranged from 0.02 to 2.82 km2 (0.65 ± 0.77 km2) with fish on average generally reaching their full MCP estimates 6 months after their first detection (Fig. 2)." (Results, p. 438 (PDF p. 6))
- Discussion restatement: "the mean MCP of less than 1 km2 (± 0.77 km2) in-combination with bonefish rarely leaving their respective flats represents one of the most extreme examples of Albula spp. having a small home range and high site fidelity." (Discussion, p. 441 to 442 (PDF p. 9 to 10))
- MCPs spanning both areas: "only 11 of the generated MCPs (constructed from COAs) spanned across Dakity and Manglar Bay (Fig. 3)" (Results, p. 438 (PDF p. 6)).

### Size effects

- Residency: "Fish size had no effect on residency (linear regression, r2 = 0.05, F1,41 = 2.19, P = 0.15)" (Results, p. 438 (PDF p. 6)).
- Dispersal: "dispersal distance (linear regression, r2 = 0.03, F1,41 = 1.70, P = 0.29)" (Results, p. 438 (PDF p. 6)).
- Home range: "or MCP size (linear regression, r2 = 0.05, F1,30 = 1.60, P = 0.22)." (Results, p. 438 (PDF p. 6))

### Movements between flats

- Detections outside the two regions: "No bonefish was ever detected outside the Dakity or Manglar Bay regions." (Results, p. 438 (PDF p. 6))
- Fish moving between flats: "examining raw detections highlighted that a total of 15 bonefish actually traveled between the two areas, ranging from 1 to 32 times (7.13 ± 7.98) (Fig. 4, Supplementary Information Figure S1)." (Results, p. 438 (PDF p. 6))
- Percentage: "Although 15 of the 43 bonefish (35%) were found to move between the reef flats of Dakity and Manglar Bay, individuals spent the majority of their time on or near their respective capture location." (Discussion, p. 439 to 440 (PDF p. 7 to 8))
- Regular movers: "Of the 15 fish that moved between Dakity and Manglar Bay (< 3 km), only three individuals did so with some regularity" (Discussion, p. 440 (PDF p. 8)).
- Seasonality: "between December 2013 and December 2014, when most tracking data occurred, movements across the two areas were generally lowest between May and September (Fig. 4b)." (Results, p. 438 to 439 (PDF p. 6 to 7))
- Seasonality restated: "movements between flats generally occurred between October and April." (Discussion, p. 440 (PDF p. 8))
- Directed movements: "Examining the movements across the two areas by individuals did not highlight any distinctive directed movements." (Results, p. 438 (PDF p. 6))

### Lunar model

- Model fit: "there was no effect of lunar cycle on the number of trips between Dakity and Manglar Bay (marginal r2 = 0.08, conditional r2 = 0.38, intraclass correlation coefficient = 0.33, Supplementary Information Table S2), the full moon stage had a P value of 0.07." (Results, p. 439 (PDF p. 7))

### Network results

- Site fidelity: "Network analysis revealed that bonefish showed high site fidelity to their respective capture areas (Fig. 5)." (Results, p. 439 (PDF p. 7))
- Dakity community: "two statistically significant regional communities associated with Dakity (Wilcoxon rank-sum test, W = 341, P < 0.001; in-degree = 40, out-degree = 15)" (Results, p. 439 (PDF p. 7)).
- Manglar Bay community: "and Manglar Bay (Wilcoxon rank-sum test, W = 472.5, P < 0.001; in-degree = 46, out-degree = 15)." (Results, p. 439 (PDF p. 7))
- Community membership: "Although 15 bonefish crossed between these two communities, all fish belonged to the regional community where they were captured and tagged." (Results, p. 439 (PDF p. 7))

### Site environment

- Tides: "tides within Culebra (mean variation = 0.21 m, max = 0.52 m) are relatively weaker than in The Bahamas (e.g., max = 0.8)" (Discussion, p. 442 (PDF p. 10)).

## Methods worth reusing

- False-detection filters: "three criteria (1) consecutive detections that were less than the minimum tag delay (45 s), (2) unrealistic movements between receivers (> 3 m per second), and (3) singular detections occurring within a specific time frame (120 min)." (Methods, Data analyses, p. 437 (PDF p. 5))
- Residency index definition: "days detected, tracking duration (time difference from the first detection to the last), residency (days detected divided by tracking duration), and dispersal distances." (Methods, Data analyses, p. 437 (PDF p. 5))
- Centres of activity: "we converted the detections into short-term centers of activity (COAs) using the mean position algorithm (Simpfendorfer et al. 2002)", "an estimated xy position for a specified time window (here, 60 min) based on the weighted means of the number of detections among each receiver" (Methods, Data analyses, p. 437 (PDF p. 5)).
- Home-range estimator: "overall and cumulative monthly space use estimates were derived from minimum convex polygons (MCPs)." (Methods, Data analyses, p. 437 (PDF p. 5))
- Minimum locations: "for bonefish with fewer than six unique locations, MCPs were unable to be estimated." (Methods, Data analyses, p. 437 (PDF p. 5))
- Land in MCPs: "MCPs could span over small areas of land that separate the two regions." (Methods, Data analyses, p. 437 (PDF p. 5))
- Zero-area correction: "considering space use MCP estimates of 0 km2 are unrealistic, we added 0.02 km2 (via π * 802) to each MCP estimate based on the approximate 50% detection efficiency at 80 m distance from receivers (Griffin et al. 2019a)." (Methods, Data analyses, p. 437 (PDF p. 5))
- Regression diagnostics: "Model assumptions were evaluated for normality and homogeneity of variance by simulating the residuals 10,000 times using the DHARMa package (Hartig 2017), and if violated, the respective dependent variable was log10 transformed." (Methods, Data analyses, p. 437 (PDF p. 5))
- Lunar model: "the relationship between the number of bonefish movements between the areas (dependent variable) and lunar cycle (independent variable), classified as new, waxing, full, or waning. This model was implemented with a generalized linear mixed model (Poisson), via the glmmTMB package (Brooks et al. 2017), using an alpha level of 0.05 and with tag ID included as a random effect to control for dependency at the level of individuals." (Methods, Data analyses, p. 437 to 438 (PDF p. 5 to 6))
- Overdispersion check: "checked for overdispersion using the performance package (Lüdecke et al. 2019)." (Methods, Data analyses, p. 438 (PDF p. 6))
- Network construction: "This approach links the movements of each tagged individual to each visited area (Dakity or Manglar Bay) by an edge (arrow) and is weighted by the number of movements/detections, thus providing an indicator of the use of each region." (Methods, Data analyses, p. 438 (PDF p. 6))
- Layout: "we applied the Fruchterman-Reingold force-directed layout algorithm (Fruchterman and Reingold 1991)." (Methods, Data analyses, p. 438 (PDF p. 6))
- Community detection: "Finn et al. (2014) demonstrated that the Fast-Greedy algorithm (Clauset et al. 2004; Newman and Girvan 2004) produced partitions that isolated reef flats where bonefish tend to spend much of their time and was the best algorithm to test our hypothesis regarding site fidelity." (Methods, Data analyses, p. 438 (PDF p. 6))
- Community confirmation: "we used a Wilcoxon rank-sum test to confirm or reject community designations (Song and Singh 2013; Finn et al. 2014). If a potential community was significant and the nodes were linked to more nodes within the group (in-degree) than other groups (out-degree), it was deemed a community." (Methods, Data analyses, p. 438 (PDF p. 6))
- Edge weighting in the figure: "the width of edges is proportional to the number of detections at each region per individual." (Fig. 5 caption, p. 442 (PDF p. 10))
- Location anonymity: "We heretofore refer to these flats only in terms of their general localities, Dakity or Manglar Bay (Fig. 1a). This approach represents best practice for ensuring that potentially sensitive data on the space use of vulnerable animals is protected (Lennox et al. 2020)." (Methods, Study sites, p. 435 (PDF p. 3))

## Management statements

- "Should future disturbance (e.g., hurricanes or netting) alter habitats or reduce one of these bonefish groups, then the recovery time on a given flat may be longer due to limited inflow of individuals." (Discussion, p. 443 (PDF p. 11))
- "considering the suggested bonefish reliance on isolated fringing reef flats in Culebra (Brownscombe et al. 2017, 2019b), protecting these productive habitats should also be prioritized." (Discussion, p. 443 (PDF p. 11))
- "sewage wastewater contamination and sediment runoff have been observed within both Dakity and Manglar Bay (Griffin et al. 2019a; Mclaughlin 2019), potentially threatening adjacent bonefish habitats (e.g., seagrass)" (Discussion, p. 443 (PDF p. 11)).
- "if larger/older individuals are lost from these isolated flats, their contribution to the spawning potential of the population could be impacted." (Discussion, p. 443 (PDF p. 11))
- "In the case of bonefish that have small home ranges and high site fidelity to small reef flats, impacts from disturbance can be especially high and result in long lasting population-level effects." (Discussion, p. 443 to 444 (PDF p. 11 to 12))
- "Even if harvest is prohibited by laws and regulations, undocumented and uncontrolled illegal harvest via gillnetting, for instance, can quickly reduce bonefish numbers." (Discussion, p. 444 (PDF p. 12))
- "the results of our study imply that a unified approach to conservation and management is needed to reduce the treats to bonefish that have a high affinity to shallow, nearshore fringing reef flats." (Discussion, p. 444 (PDF p. 12))

## Stated limitations

- "As with all studies with acoustic telemetry, our conclusions could be influenced by the distribution of the receivers, temporal coverage/consistency in detection histories, as well as where bonefish were tagged (Heupel et al. 2006; Brownscombe et al. 2019c)." (Discussion, p. 443 (PDF p. 11))
- "If we had the capacity to deploy additional receivers for our study, it may have been prudent to create small curtains to better capture movement patterns to and from fringing reef flats and to identify spawning migrations." (Discussion, p. 443 (PDF p. 11))
- "While we were unable to identify any distinctive spawning migrations" (Discussion, p. 441 (PDF p. 9)).
- "occasional detection gaps occurred across multiple days or even months for multiple fish (Fig. 4a)." (Results, p. 438 (PDF p. 6))
- "For bonefish with greater than six unique locations, it should be noted that MCPs could span over small areas of land that separate the two regions." (Methods, Data analyses, p. 437 (PDF p. 5))
- "The datasets generated during and/or analyzed during the current study are not publicly available due to sensitive fishery data but are available from the corresponding author on reasonable request." (Data availability, p. 444 (PDF p. 12))

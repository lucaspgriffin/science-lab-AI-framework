# Reef flat site fidelity

## Summary

Griffin et al. (2023) used a fixed-station acoustic telemetry array to follow 43 bonefish (*Albula
vulpes*) on two small fringing reef flats in Culebra, Puerto Rico, from July 2012 to June 2015
[Griffin et al. 2023, pp. 435, 438]. Site fidelity was measured three ways: a residency index (days
detected divided by tracking duration), dispersal distance from the tagging location, and minimum
convex polygon (MCP) home ranges built from hourly centres of activity. A network analysis tested
whether fish formed communities tied to their tagging flat. Mean residency was 0.78 (± 0.25),
dispersal never exceeded 3.52 km, and mean MCP area was 0.65 (± 0.77) km² [Griffin et al. 2023, p.
438]. No fish was detected outside the two flat regions, and every fish belonged to the network
community of its capture area [Griffin et al. 2023, pp. 438–439]. The authors describe the result as
"one of the most extreme examples of *Albula* spp. having a small home range and high site fidelity"
[Griffin et al. 2023, pp. 441–442], with small isolated groups vulnerable to local impacts.

## Key points

### Study system

Culebra is a small island (28 km²) whose shallow reef flats (< 2 m deep) are separated by deeper
channels of 8–10 m [Griffin et al. 2023, p. 435]. The study concentrated on two flats, referred to
only by their general localities, Dakity and Manglar Bay, with seagrass, sand, coral rubble,
macroalgae, and hard bottom [Griffin et al. 2023, p. 435]. Tides are weak: mean variation is 0.21 m
and the maximum is 0.52 m, compared with a maximum of about 0.8 m in The Bahamas [Griffin et al.
2023, p. 442]. Weak tides bear on interpretation, because bonefish movement patterns are "largely
dominated by tidal flows, thermal regime, and seasonal spawning migrations" [Griffin et al. 2023, p.
433].

### Sample and tracking

Of the 43 fish, 20 were tagged at Dakity and 23 at Manglar Bay [Griffin et al. 2023, p. 435]. Mean
size was 522 ± 56 mm, and fish tagged at Manglar Bay (540 ± 57 mm) were significantly larger than
those tagged at Dakity (501 ± 48 mm) [Griffin et al. 2023, p. 438]. Mean tracking duration was 412
(± 237) days [Griffin et al. 2023, p. 438].

### Residency and dispersal

Overall residency was 0.78 (± 0.25) [Griffin et al. 2023, p. 438]. Dispersal distances from the
tagging location ranged from 0 to 3.52 km, with a mean of 1.42 ± 1.08 km [Griffin et al. 2023, p.
438], against a mean mark-to-recapture distance of 3.5–11.1 km in The Bahamas (Boucek et al. 2019)
[Griffin et al. 2023, p. 442].

### Home range

Thirty-two fish had enough locations for an MCP estimate [Griffin et al. 2023, p. 438]. Estimates
ranged from 0.02 to 2.82 km², with a mean of 0.65 ± 0.77 km², and fish on average reached their full
home range about 6 months after first detection [Griffin et al. 2023, p. 438]. Only 11 MCPs spanned
both Dakity and Manglar Bay [Griffin et al. 2023, p. 438]. The nearest comparison cited in the paper
is an Indian Ocean atoll, where acoustic telemetry gave a mean MCP of 5.42 (± 1.85) km² (Moxham et
al. 2019) [Griffin et al. 2023, p. 442].

Fish size predicted none of the three fidelity metrics. Linear regressions gave r² = 0.05, F1,41 =
2.19, P = 0.15 for residency; r² = 0.03, F1,41 = 1.70, P = 0.29 for dispersal distance; and r² =
0.05, F1,30 = 1.60, P = 0.22 for MCP area [Griffin et al. 2023, p. 438].

### Movement between flats

Raw detections showed that 15 of the 43 fish (35%) travelled between the two flats, making 1 to 32
crossings each (7.13 ± 7.98) [Griffin et al. 2023, pp. 438–440]. Only three of these fish crossed
with any regularity, and the distance between the flats is under 3 km [Griffin et al. 2023, p. 440].
Individual crossings showed no distinctive directed movements [Griffin et al. 2023, p. 438]. Between
December 2013 and December 2014, when most tracking data were collected, crossings were lowest from
May to September, and the Discussion states that movements between flats "generally occurred between
October and April" [Griffin et al. 2023, pp. 438–440]. Lunar phase had no effect on the number of
crossings. The Poisson mixed model returned a marginal r² of 0.08, a conditional r² of 0.38, and an
intraclass correlation coefficient of 0.33; the full moon stage had P = 0.07 [Griffin et al. 2023,
p. 439].

### Network structure

Network analysis recovered two significant regional communities. Dakity gave W = 341, P < 0.001,
in-degree 40, out-degree 15; Manglar Bay gave W = 472.5, P < 0.001, in-degree 46, out-degree 15
[Griffin et al. 2023, p. 439]. "Although 15 bonefish crossed between these two communities, all fish
belonged to the regional community where they were captured and tagged" [Griffin et al. 2023, p.
439].

### Management implications

The authors warn that "should future disturbance (e.g., hurricanes or netting) alter habitats or
reduce one of these bonefish groups, then the recovery time on a given flat may be longer due to
limited inflow of individuals" [Griffin et al. 2023, p. 443]. Sewage contamination and sediment
runoff have been observed at both flats [Griffin et al. 2023, p. 443]. "Even if harvest is
prohibited by laws and regulations, undocumented and uncontrolled illegal harvest via gillnetting,
for instance, can quickly reduce bonefish numbers" [Griffin et al. 2023, p. 444]. The paper
concludes that habitat protection should be prioritised and that "a unified approach to conservation
and management is needed" for bonefish with high affinity to fringing reef flats [Griffin et al.
2023, pp. 443–444].

## Methods and approaches

### Array design

The array held 59 VR2W receivers, of which 25 formed a Vemco Positioning System sub-array within
Manglar Bay [Griffin et al. 2023, p. 435]. For movement analysis the positioning receivers were
collapsed into two nodes: 17 lagoon receivers formed one node and 8 reef receivers formed another.
This left 36 distinct locations covering about 40 km², land included [Griffin et al. 2023, p. 435].

### Detection range

Detection range comes from range testing of the same array in Griffin et al. (2019a), which found
that "our shallow water flats receivers had 50% detection efficiency at a radius of 80 m" [Griffin
et al. 2023, p. 435]. That radius sets the floor added to every home range.

### Tagging

Two transmitter types were used: V13-1L coded tags (6.0 g in air, 45–135 s delay, 880-day battery)
and V13AP accelerometer tags (12.2 g, 45–135 s delay, ± 3.43 g range, 5 Hz sampling, 323-day
battery) [Griffin et al. 2023, p. 435]. Fish were caught on fly or spinning gear, and only fish of
410 mm or more were tagged [Griffin et al. 2023, pp. 435–436]. Surgery under MS-222 took under 5 min
[Griffin et al. 2023, pp. 436–437].

### Detection filtering

False detections were removed under "three criteria (1) consecutive detections that were less than
the minimum tag delay (45 s), (2) unrealistic movements between receivers (> 3 m per second), and
(3) singular detections occurring within a specific time frame (120 min)" [Griffin et al. 2023, p.
437].

### Residency metrics

Per-fish summaries were days detected, tracking duration ("time difference from the first detection
to the last"), residency ("days detected divided by tracking duration"), and dispersal distance
[Griffin et al. 2023, p. 437].

### Home-range estimation

Detections were converted to short-term centres of activity with the mean position algorithm of
Simpfendorfer et al. (2002), giving "an estimated xy position for a specified time window (here, 60
min) based on the weighted means of the number of detections among each receiver" [Griffin et al.
2023, p. 437]. VTrack (Campbell et al. 2012) is among the packages named in the analyses [Griffin et
al. 2023, pp. 437–438].

Overall and cumulative monthly space use were estimated as MCPs from the centres of activity
[Griffin et al. 2023, p. 437]. Three settings govern reuse:

- **Minimum locations.** Fish with fewer than six unique locations received no MCP [Griffin et al.
  2023, p. 437], which is why 32 of 43 fish have estimates.
- **Zero-area floor.** Because MCPs of 0 km² are unrealistic, "we added 0.02 km² (via π * 80²) to
  each MCP estimate based on the approximate 50% detection efficiency at 80 m distance from
  receivers (Griffin et al. 2019a)" [Griffin et al. 2023, p. 437]. The smallest reported MCP, 0.02
  km², is therefore the floor itself.
- **Land.** MCPs could span small areas of land separating the two regions [Griffin et al. 2023, p.
  437], so the upper estimates include some unusable area.

### Statistical models

Analyses ran in R 3.6.2, with means reported ± one standard deviation [Griffin et al. 2023, p. 437].
Linear regressions tested size against residency, dispersal, and MCP area. Regression residuals were
simulated 10,000 times with DHARMa, and the response was log10 transformed if normality or
homogeneity of variance failed [Griffin et al. 2023, p. 437]. Crossings between flats were modelled
as a Poisson generalised linear mixed model in glmmTMB, with lunar phase (new, waxing, full, waning)
as the fixed effect, tag ID as a random effect, and α = 0.05; overdispersion was checked with the
performance package [Griffin et al. 2023, pp. 437–438].

### Network analysis

Networks were built in igraph, linking each fish to each visited area by an edge "weighted by the
number of movements/detections" [Griffin et al. 2023, p. 438]. Communities were found with the
Fast-Greedy algorithm, chosen because Finn et al. (2014) showed it isolated reef flats where
bonefish spend much of their time [Griffin et al. 2023, p. 438]. A Wilcoxon rank-sum test then
confirmed or rejected each candidate: "If a potential community was significant and the nodes were
linked to more nodes within the group (in-degree) than other groups (out-degree), it was deemed a
community" [Griffin et al. 2023, p. 438].

### Location anonymity

Flats are named only by general locality, which the paper calls "best practice for ensuring that
potentially sensitive data on the space use of vulnerable animals is protected" (Lennox et al. 2020)
[Griffin et al. 2023, p. 435].

## Open questions

- Receiver placement, temporal coverage, and tagging location could all shape the conclusions
  [Griffin et al. 2023, p. 443]. The authors suggest that small receiver curtains would have better
  captured movements to and from the flats and any spawning migration [Griffin et al. 2023, p. 443].
- No spawning migration was detected, so the link between these resident groups and offshore
  spawning sites remains open [Griffin et al. 2023, p. 441].
- Minimum convex polygons describe extent, not intensity of use. Re-estimating these home ranges
  with an autocorrelation-aware estimator would test whether the small-range result holds under a
  utilisation-distribution approach.

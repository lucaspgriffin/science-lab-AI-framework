# Griffin et al. 2024

Running head on every page reads "Can. J. Fish. Aquat. Sci. 82: 1–18 (2025)"; the copyright line reads "© 2024 The Author(s)" and "Version of record online: 12 December 2024" (p. 15 (PDF p. 15)). Journal page numbers equal PDF page numbers throughout.

## Verbatim abstract

> "By combining acoustic telemetry, mark-and-recapture, and location-specific catch logs, we tested whether angler-fish interactions altered the behavior, space use, and catchability of giant trevally (Caranx ignobilis) (GT) in coastal waters of the Alphonse Island Group, Seychelles, Indian Ocean. Adult GT near a high human use area with limited provisioning showed restricted movements during the tourism fishing season and had home ranges nearly 50% smaller than that of adult GT from other parts of the atoll group. Despite angler catch maps overlapping with 30% of non-habituated adult GT core use areas, recaptures were infrequent, suggesting that GT became hook shy. Although there was no change in movement patterns during a temporary fishing closure caused by COVID-19, non-habituated GT movement patterns shifted toward wider ranging movements with each successive fishing season, potentially to avoid shallow water areas where angler activities occurred. Our findings highlight how angler interactions and catch-and-release fisheries have the potential to modify the behavior, space use, and catchability of targeted species, and may influence the viability of the fishery by exceeding its capacity." (Abstract, p. 1 (PDF p. 1))

## Study system and design

- Species: "giant trevally (Caranx ignobilis) (GT)" (Abstract, p. 1 (PDF p. 1)).
- Site: "The Alphonse Group (study area: −7.05, 52.73) is comprised of two low-lying coralline atolls, Alphonse and St. François atolls, which are separated by a 2.5 km wide channel." (2.1 Study area, p. 3 (PDF p. 3)).
- Site setting: "located 87 km south of the Amirantes Ridge"; "Depths reach >2000 m adjacent (7–10 km) to the coral plateaus."; "the Alphonse Island (174 ha)" (2.1, p. 3 (PDF p. 3)).
- Dock: "a floating dock (62 m in length, −7.00911, 52.73087) used for loading/unloading guests and activities equipment" (2.1, p. 3 (PDF p. 3)).
- Habitat extents: "Both Alphonse and St. François atolls have extensive shallow (<10 m) lagoons (540 and 1650 ha, respectively) and peripheral reef flats (402 and 3732 ha, respectively) consisting of sand, coral rubble, and seagrass" (2.1, p. 3 (PDF p. 3)).
- Isolation: "Given the geographic isolation of the Alphonse Group (150 km from the nearest atoll)" (Discussion, p. 14 (PDF p. 14)).
- Receiver array: "Sixty-eight autonomous fixed acoustic receivers (VR2W; Innovasea, Boston, MA) were originally deployed on the seafloor (1.5–11 m) around and within the atolls" (2.2, p. 3 (PDF p. 3)).
- Dock station group: "data from seven receivers located around the dock were aggregated into a single station group" (2.2, p. 3 (PDF p. 3)).
- Range test design: "three reference tags positioned 75, 150, and 300 m away from an acoustic receiver within the Alphonse Atoll" (2.2, p. 3 (PDF p. 3)).
- Tagging period: "GT to be tracked via acoustic telemetry were caught using recreational fishing gear (fly and conventional tackle) between October 2018 and December 2019." (2.2, p. 3 (PDF p. 3)).
- Transmitter models: "ping-only functionality (V16-4x; high power, 60–120 s delay, 1910-day life, 24 g, 16 × 68 mm, Amirix), ping with depth sensor (V16P-4x; high power, 60–120 s delay, 1715-day life, 1-36 m, 24 g, 16 × 68 mm, Amirix), and ping with acceleration/depth sensor functionality (V13AP-1x; high power, 60–120 s delay, 358-day life, 1-136 m, 9.2 g, 13 × 30.5 mm, Amirix)" (2.2, p. 3 (PDF p. 3)). The V13AP depth range of 1-136 m is split at a line break after the hyphen.
- Accelerometer tags: "tags with acceleration sensors (n = 12) allowed for the comparison of activity estimates across individuals and seasons" (2.2, p. 3 (PDF p. 3)).
- Acceleration sampling: "Acceleration (±4.9 m s−2 SE range) is measured and calculated from three orthogonal axes, which are sampled for each transmission cycle (60–120 s) at 5 Hz for 5 s" (2.2, p. 3 (PDF p. 3)).
- Surgery: "We inserted acoustic transmitters into the coelomic cavity after making 3–4 cm incision with a scalpel through the muscle wall. The incision was closed using three interrupted sutures (PDS-II monofilament absorbable, 3-0, model Z497G; Ethicon)." (2.2, p. 3 (PDF p. 3)).
- Ethics: "approved by UMass IACUC, protocol 2016-0049, and under a research permit from the Seychelles Bureau of Standards" (2.2, p. 3 (PDF p. 3)).
- Data sharing: "All data were uploaded into the Ocean Tracking Network" (2.2, p. 3 (PDF p. 3)).
- Catch logs: "Beginning in January 2017, GT catch locations and associated data were recorded with Geographic Positioning System (GPS) watches (Garmin Quatix III GPS watches, Garmin United States, 15 m accuracy)." (2.3, p. 3 (PDF p. 3)).
- PIT tags: "in October 2018, PIT (12.5 mm, ATP12, Biomark Inc., Boise, Idaho) were implanted"; "inserted into the dorsal musculature using an individually loaded MK15 PIT tag implanter" (2.3, p. 3 (PDF p. 3)).
- Who tagged: "GT were primarily captured by client anglers and tagged by an accompanied fishing guide." (2.3, p. 3 (PDF p. 3)).
- Software: "All statistical analyses were conducted using R 4.2.2 and R Studio 2022.12.0 + 353 (R Core Team 2022)."; packages "dplyr", "lubridate", "data.table", "raster", "viridis", "ggplot2", "ggmap", and "tmap" (2.4, p. 3 (PDF p. 3)).
- Reporting: "all summary statistics below were reported as mean ± standard deviation, and all statistical tests were implemented with an alpha level set to 0.05" (2.4, p. 3 (PDF p. 3)).
- Analysis packages: "glatos" (residency, detection events), "VTrack" (COAs), "adehabitatHR" (KUDs), "classInt" (Fisher thresholds), "glmmTMB" (GLMMs), "DHARMA", "sjplot", "lme4" (random effects), "pROC" (AUC) (2.4.2 to 2.4.4, pp. 4-5 (PDF pp. 4-5)).
- Hypotheses tested: habituation, closure effect (COVID-19), timidity (1 Introduction, p. 2 (PDF p. 2)).

## Methods worth reusing

- False-detection filters: "(1) consecutive detections that took place within a time frame less than 60 s (minimum tag delay), (2) unrealistic detections (movements) that were above 10 m s−1, as derived from straight-line distances (a higher rate was used when considering variable detection ranges), and (3) singular detections occurring within a specific time frame (defined as 120 min)." (2.4.1, p. 4 (PDF p. 4)).
- Mortality and emigration screen: "we examined and plotted the detection logs of all fish with 25 or less of monitoring days (calculated as the duration between the release date and last detection date), days detected (calculated as number of unique detection days), or those with detections on five or fewer unique stations" (2.4.1, p. 4 (PDF p. 4)).
- Detection events: "Using the detection_events function in the glatos package (Holbrook et al. 2020), this function organizes detections into separate events based on when an individual is detected at a different location or when a certain threshold is reached (here, defined as 60 min)." (2.4.2, p. 4 (PDF p. 4)).
- Residency index: "The residence_index function (Kessel method: residence index equal to the distinct number of days detected at the location divided by the distinct number of days detected at any location) in the glatos package" (2.4.2, p. 4 (PDF p. 4)).
- Habituation rule: "Any dock residency greater than 0.5 was considered “habituated”." (2.4.2, p. 4 (PDF p. 4)).
- Adult and juvenile split at 60 cm FL (2.4.2, p. 4 (PDF p. 4)).
- COAs: "short-term centers of activity (COAs) using the mean position algorithm (Simpfendorfer et al. 2002) with 60 min time bins through the VTrack package" (2.4.3, p. 4 (PDF p. 4)).
- KUDs: "we constructed kernel utilization distributions (KUDs) using 200 m smoothing bands and the adehabitatHR package (Calenge 2006). KDEs (50%, 95%) were then extracted from each KUD" (2.4.3, p. 4 (PDF p. 4)).
- Activity tests: "we conducted a Welch two-sample t test"; seasons defined as "the fishing season (September–May) and the off-season (June–August)" (2.4.3, p. 4 (PDF p. 4)).
- Gap analysis recursion filter: "we eliminated detection gaps of less than 6 min to ensure that the recursion took place and to remove potential false positives" (2.4.4, p. 4 (PDF p. 4)).
- Clustering: "clustered into either restricted or out-of-range movement types using a threshold as determined by the classIntervals function with the Fisher algorithm (Fisher 1958) in the classInt package" (2.4.4, p. 4 (PDF p. 4)).
- RDET: "the expected time difference between detections was calculated as the distance between each recursion divided by the average swim speed (m per second) of a GT. The mean swim speed was determined by the 12 GT that were tagged with acceleration transmitters."; "Movements that were greater than five times the expected speed (RDET > 5) were removed" (2.4.4, pp. 4-5 (PDF pp. 4-5)).
- GLMM structure: "generalized linear model (GLMM, family = binomial, link = logit) with movement type as the response variable, fish classification and fishing season (during or outside) as an interaction term, and two separate random effects; individual and station location." (2.4.4, p. 5 (PDF p. 5)).
- Train and test split: "trained on 60% of the data"; "we tested the predictive capability on the remaining 40% of the holdout dataset. The area under the curve (AUC) was then calculated using the pROC package" (2.4.4, p. 5 (PDF p. 5)).
- Residual checks: "by plotting simulated residuals (10 000 times) using the DHARMA package (Hartig 2017)" (2.4.4, p. 5 (PDF p. 5)).
- Closure periods: "April–May across 2019 (pre-COVID-19), 2020 (during COVID-19, set as reference level in GLMM), and 2021 (post-COVID-19)" (2.4.5, p. 5 (PDF p. 5)).
- Length classes: "small adults (60–100 cm FL) or large adults (>100 cm FL) (Daly et al. 2021)"; "lengths were recalculated, and length classifications were re-classified throughout the monitoring period according to the growth curve defined for GT by Sudekum (1991)" (2.4.5, p. 5 (PDF p. 5)).
- Nearest station distance covariate: "computed by constructing nearest distance matrices for each station-year combination" (2.4.5, p. 5 (PDF p. 5)).
- Timidity seasons: "Fishing season was defined as September 2019–May 2020 (set as reference level in GLMM), September 2020–May 2021, and September 2021–May 2022." (2.4.6, p. 5 (PDF p. 5)).
- Catch smoothing: "The catch data was smoothed with a 200 m parameter to account for location uncertainty, especially when no waypoint was taken and locations were derived from general fishing locations." (2.4.7, p. 5 (PDF p. 5)).
- Overlap metric: "The total areas encompassed by the 95% and 50% KDEs for catch data and GT space use were calculated and the percentage overlap by the catch data to GT space use was evaluated." (2.4.7, p. 5 (PDF p. 5)).

## Management statements

- "Collectively, our findings suggest that the fishery capacity within the Alphonse Group may be at saturation, characterized by a sustained level of hook shyness in GT, which is unlikely to change without the introduction of new individuals to the population or reduction in fishing pressure." (Discussion, p. 14 (PDF p. 14)).
- "The initial precautionary management strategies enacted by Alphonse Fishing Company, e.g., limiting the number of anglers per day and strict handling guidelines (see Griffin et al. 2021), have likely helped in achieving and balancing angler pressure and satisfaction." (Discussion, p. 14 (PDF p. 14)).
- "Because catchability is often driven by species-specific differences and angler behavior (Yoshiyama et al. 2023), broadly focusing on multiple species may extend the fishing capacity for GT." (Discussion, p. 14 (PDF p. 14)).
- "For future studies and continued monitoring, catch-per-unit effort should be evaluated for each species and fishery alongside the methodologies presented in our study." (Discussion, pp. 14-15 (PDF pp. 14-15)).
- "Consequently, further biological and ecological investigations into GT spawning behaviors and larval recruitment dynamics in this region are warranted." (Discussion, p. 15 (PDF p. 15)).
- "This observed shift in behavior further suggests that the capacity of the fishery may have peaked, meaning that adding angler pressure may lead to greater spatial avoidance and hook shyness." (Conclusion, p. 15 (PDF p. 15)).
- "This highlights the need for additional research to determine the minimum duration of fishing closure that would be needed to elicit observable shifts in GT behavior." (Discussion, p. 14 (PDF p. 14)).

## Stated limitations

- Detection range: "yet was likely lower along the reef, where higher rugosity and environmental/biological noise exist." (2.2, p. 3 (PDF p. 3)).
- Array changes: "receiver losses incurred, specifically around the southeastern corner of St. François Atoll, and minor modification of the array and redeployment of receivers were required annually." (2.2, p. 3 (PDF p. 3)).
- Habituation attribution: "Although this behavior cannot be definitively attributed to food provisioning, especially since provisioning in recent years has been limited, and GT may be attracted to structure or influenced by seasonal and other environmental factors" (Discussion, p. 12 (PDF p. 12)).
- Open system: "the Alphonse Group is an open system that allows for immigration and emigration of fish." (Discussion, p. 14 (PDF p. 14)).
- Hook shyness claims: "it is important to consider that claims of hook shyness might be overstated to rationalize unsuccessful fishing." (Discussion, p. 14 (PDF p. 14)).
- Unmeasured factors: "One potential additional factor not explored in this study is the effect of fish satiation or body condition on capture and recapture vulnerability." (Discussion, p. 14 (PDF p. 14)).
- Population size: "A larger GT population would inherently decrease the likelihood of recapturing individual fish." (Discussion, p. 14 (PDF p. 14)).
- Effort and receiver bias: "This distribution in angling effort and depth limitations to deploy acoustic telemetry receivers may bias home range estimates and the percentage of overlap between anglers and GT." (Discussion, p. 14 (PDF p. 14)).
- Catch log coverage: "Only 831 GT PIT tagged had corresponding capture data recorded as part of the guide catch logs." (3.7, p. 11 (PDF p. 11)).

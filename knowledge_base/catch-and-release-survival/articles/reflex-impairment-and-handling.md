# Reflex impairment and handling

## Summary

Griffin et al. (2022) tested how capture and handling affect giant trevally (*Caranx ignobilis*) caught on fly gear in
the lagoon of Alphonse Atoll, Republic of Seychelles. Fifty fish were caught, 36 by fly and 14 by heavy handline as a
quickly landed reference [Griffin et al. 2022, p. 4]. Fly-caught fish were assigned to 0, 10, or 30 s of air exposure,
and handlined fish were not exposed to air [Griffin et al. 2022, p. 3]. Response was measured with a five-reflex
battery scored as a proportion and with tri-axial accelerometers tethered to the fish for 15 min after release
[Griffin et al. 2022, p. 3]. Neither fight time nor air exposure had a detectable effect on reflex impairment or on
overall dynamic body acceleration, and the only potential mortality was a handlined fish deeply hooked near the gills
[Griffin et al. 2022, pp. 1, 5]. The study reports no blood chemistry (lactate, glucose, or cortisol); stress was
indexed by reflexes and accelerometry alone. The authors framed the result as a best case for careful in-water
handling by experienced guides, and some of the paper's printed counts for hooking injury do not agree with one
another (see Reporting inconsistencies).

## Key points

### Study design

Sampling ran opportunistically from November 2019 to March 2021 in a lagoon of "approximately 26–31∘C water
temperature" [Griffin et al. 2022, p. 2]. Fly gear was a 12 wt rod with "single hook barbless flies 6/0–10/0", and the
reference handline was 113 kg monofilament with barbed 20/0 circle hooks baited with fish carcasses [Griffin et al.
2022, p. 2]. Group sizes were 14 handline at 0 s, 11 fly at 0 s, 13 fly at 10 s, and 12 fly at 30 s [Griffin et al.
2022, p. 4, Table 1]. Fork length ranged from 63.5 to 128 cm in fly-caught fish (97.1 ± 14.3 cm) and from 77 to 108 cm
in handlined fish (94.4 ± 9.6 cm), with no difference between gears (t(35.52) = −0.79, p = 0.44) or among air exposure
treatments (F(2, 47) = 0.11, p = 0.9) [Griffin et al. 2022, p. 4]. Predator density at the site "was assumed to be low
with rare sightings of large sharks" [Griffin et al. 2022, p. 2].

### Fight time

Handlined fish were landed in 82 ± 48 s, against 453 ± 267 s for fly-caught fish (W = 10, p < 0.001) [Griffin et al.
2022, p. 4]. Fight times did not differ among the three fly treatments (463 ± 272 s at 0 s of air, 433 ± 258 s at 10
s, and 466 ± 292 s at 30 s; H(2) = 0.13, p = 0.94) [Griffin et al. 2022, p. 4]. Fight time rose with body size for
both handlined (r(12) = 0.58, p = 0.03) and fly-caught fish (r(34) = 0.54, p < 0.001) [Griffin et al. 2022, p. 4].

### Hooking and injury

Most fish were hooked in the jaw or corner of the mouth: 30 of 36 fly-caught fish (83.3%) and 12 of 14 handlined fish
(85.7%) [Griffin et al. 2022, pp. 4–5]. Among fly-caught fish, two were hooked in the roof of the mouth (5.6%), one in
the tongue (2.8%), and three deep in the back of the mouth (8.3%); among handlined fish, one was hooked in the tongue
(7.1%) and one deep in the back of the mouth (7.1%) [Griffin et al. 2022, pp. 4–5]. Hooking impact was scored from
hook removal difficulty in three classes: "not difficult with minimal hook removal time (< 5 s), moderately difficult
with additional hook removal time (≥ 5 and < 30 s), or very difficult with excessive hook removal time (≥ 30 s)",
mapped to mild, moderate, and severe [Griffin et al. 2022, p. 3]. Mild impact was recorded for 28 fly-caught fish
(77.8%) and 12 handlined fish (85.7%), and moderate impact for seven fly-caught (19.4%) and one handlined fish (7.1%)
[Griffin et al. 2022, p. 5]. The authors concluded that "deep hooking is likely negligible when fly fishing for GT due
to a combination of active hook setting by anglers and because of how GTs aggressively strike flies" [Griffin et al.
2022, p. 6].

### Reflex battery

The battery scored five reflexes, each as present (1) or absent (0) [Griffin et al. 2022, p. 3]:

- **Head complex**: "the presence of steady operculum beats during handling".
- **Vestibular ocular response**: "the tracking and rolling of the eye as the orientation of the fish changes".
- **Body flex**: "the presence of flexion in the torso (within 3 s) when a fish is held along the dorsoventral axis".
- **Tail grab**: "the presence of burst swimming action when a fish is grabbed by the caudal peduncle (within 3
  attempts)".
- **Equilibrium**: "the ability of the fish to right itself within three seconds after being placed upside down in
  water".

Scores were converted "to a proportional RAMP impairment score ranging from 0 to 1, where a cumulative score of 1
indicated no overall impairment and a score of 0 indicated total reflex impairment" [Griffin et al. 2022, p. 3]. The
scale runs opposite to impairment: a higher score means a less impaired fish.

### Reflex results

Proportional scores ranged from 0.4 to 1.00 (0.72 ± 0.11) across all fish [Griffin et al. 2022, p. 5]. Handlined (0.71
± 0.13) and fly-caught fish (0.72 ± 0.11) did not differ (W = 225, p = 0.95), nor did handlined fish and fly-caught
fish held at 0 s of air (W = 62.5, p = 0.33) [Griffin et al. 2022, p. 5]. Group means fell from 0.76 at 0 s to 0.74 at
10 s and 0.67 at 30 s of air [Griffin et al. 2022, p. 4, Table 1]. The authors reported "only weak evidence that air
exposure had an effect on RAMP (H(36) = 5.2, p = 0.07, eta-squared = 0.1)" [Griffin et al. 2022, p. 5]. Among fish
with no air exposure, fight time had no effect on the score (z = 0.57, p = 0.57) [Griffin et al. 2022, p. 5].

The reflexes did not fail evenly. Tail grab (0.08 ± 0.27) and body flex (0.54 ± 0.5) were most often impaired, while
head complex and vestibular ocular response never failed [Griffin et al. 2022, p. 5]. Body flex was present in 0.73 of
fish at 0 s, 0.54 at 10 s, and 0.25 at 30 s, and tail grab in 0.09, 0.15, and 0.00 [Griffin et al. 2022, p. 4, Table
1]. Equilibrium failed only once, in a 108 cm handlined fish "hooked deeply near the gills with a hooking impact score
noted as "severe"" [Griffin et al. 2022, p. 5].

### Post-release activity

Mean overall dynamic body acceleration ranged from 0.18 to 1.49 g (0.89 ± 0.3 g) [Griffin et al. 2022, pp. 5–6].
Activity did not differ between handlined (1.03 ± 0.32 g) and fly-caught fish (0.85 ± 0.29 g; t(12.51) = 1.5, p =
0.16) or among fly air exposure groups (F(2, 24) = 1.09, p = 0.35) [Griffin et al. 2022, p. 6]. Activity was not
correlated with reflex score in handlined (r(9) = 0.35, p = 0.35) or fly-caught fish (r(27) = −0.4, p = 0.85) [Griffin
et al. 2022, p. 6]. Fly-caught fish were less active in the first minutes after release: values at 1–3 min were lower
than at 7–9 min (z = −3.35, p = 0.01), 10–12 min (z = −4.37, p < 0.001), and 13–15 min (z = −3.77, p = 0.01) [Griffin
et al. 2022, p. 6]. Handlined fish showed no change over the period (F(4, 30.03) = 0.47, p = 0.75) [Griffin et al.
2022, p. 6].

### Mortality

One of 50 fish (2%) was a potential short-term mortality, the deeply hooked handlined fish that lost equilibrium and
had the lowest reflex score (0.4) [Griffin et al. 2022, pp. 1, 5]. The one other deeply hooked fish named in the
Results was fly-caught (86 cm), scored 0.6, and was pursued by a lemon shark after release without an observed
predation event; it was the only predator encounter among the 50 releases [Griffin et al. 2022, pp. 5–6].

### Handling recommendations

The authors concluded that "under the conditions tested, GTs are relatively resilient to the negative effects often
imposed by capture and handling" and that "short-term effects of air exposure and extended fight times are minimal"
[Griffin et al. 2022, p. 6]. The general recommendation is that "anglers should adopt or continue to use best handling
practices, e.g., minimizing air exposure and handling time" [Griffin et al. 2022, p. 7]. For the Alphonse Island
Group, best handling practices were recommended "to potentially help recovery time and to mitigate hook learning or
timidness" [Griffin et al. 2022, p. 8]. Three cautions limit transfer to other fisheries. Bait fisheries that rely on
passive approaches "may result in higher frequencies of deep hooking and instances of mortality" [Griffin et al. 2022,
p. 6]. Treble hooked lures were not evaluated and are "likely to become embedded in sensitive locations that may be
detrimental to fish survival" [Griffin et al. 2022, p. 7]. If predator burdens rise, "GT may be susceptible to
predation events regardless of their resiliency to the effects of C&R angling" [Griffin et al. 2022, p. 6]. The
authors also suggested that "rotating, displacing, or reducing angling pressure" may be needed for long-term
sustainability [Griffin et al. 2022, p. 7].

### Reporting inconsistencies

Printed counts disagree in six places. The Abstract gives the air exposure treatments as 0, 15, and 30 s, whereas the
Methods, Table 1, and the appendix tables give 0, 10, and 30 s [Griffin et al. 2022, pp. 1, 3, 4, 8]; the 10 s figure
appears in every place the data are reported. The severe hooking impact sentence states that "only two ranked as
severe" but then lists one fly-caught fish (5.6%) and two handlined fish (14.3%), which sums to three [Griffin et al.
2022, p. 5]. One of 36 is 2.8%, not 5.6%. With two severe cases, the handlined mild, moderate, and severe counts (12,
1, and 2) sum to 15 of 14 fish, and only one handlined fish was recorded as deeply hooked [Griffin et al. 2022, pp.
4–5]. The hooking location counts list three deeply hooked fly-caught fish, yet the Results describe a single other
deeply hooked fish after the handlined mortality, and the Abstract reports one fish "hooked in a critical location for
each capture gear" [Griffin et al. 2022, pp. 1, 4, 5]. The correlation degrees of freedom for activity against reflex
score imply 11 handlined and 29 fly-caught fish, while the appendix activity models use 27 fly-caught fish [Griffin et
al. 2022, pp. 6, 8]. None of these discrepancies changes the direction of the main findings, but counts from this
paper should be quoted with the discrepancy noted.

## Methods and approaches

**Capture and holding.** Experienced guides fished from a ~5 m vessel, and fish were held submerged in a mesh cradle
for processing [Griffin et al. 2022, pp. 2–3]. Fight time was the time "from hooking to landing" [Griffin et al. 2022,
p. 3].

**Accelerometry.** A Gulf Coast Data Concepts X16-mini logger was attached to the caudal peduncle with elastic bands
in under 30 s, and tethered to a rod and reel (54 kg braid) left in free spool during monitoring [Griffin et al. 2022,
p. 3]. After 15 min, drag was applied to break the elastics and recover the logger [Griffin et al. 2022, p. 3]. Four
trials ended early when the tether caught on coral heads [Griffin et al. 2022, p. 3]. Raw values were divided by 2048
to convert to g, static acceleration was estimated per axis with a 2 s box smoother (`zoo::rollmean`), and overall
dynamic body acceleration was the sum of absolute dynamic acceleration across the three axes, averaged per minute
after release [Griffin et al. 2022, p. 3].

**Group comparisons.** Body size was compared between gears with a Welch t-test and among air exposure treatments with
one-way ANOVA; fight times were compared with Wilcoxon rank-sum tests and, among fly treatments, a Kruskal-Wallis test
[Griffin et al. 2022, p. 3]. Assumptions were checked following Zuur et al. (2010), and non-parametric tests were used
where they failed [Griffin et al. 2022, p. 3]. **Reflex models.** Proportional scores for fly-caught fish were
modelled with binomial generalized linear models in `glmmTMB` [Griffin et al. 2022, p. 3]. Air exposure entered either
at treatment level (0, 10, 30 s) or as a binary exposed or not exposed factor, and each coding was fitted as fight
time alone, air alone, additive, and interaction models, with Akaike information criterion used for covariate
selection [Griffin et al. 2022, p. 3]. All models fit about equally poorly. The binary air-only model had the lowest
value (82.558), followed by fight time alone (82.990) and treatment-level air alone (83.786), and marginal R² never
exceeded 0.017 [Griffin et al. 2022, p. 8, Table A1]. Estimates in Table A1 are printed so that 1.00 means no effect
and values below 1 carry negative z (for example, air at 30 s, 0.62, z = −1.14, p = 0.253), which reads as odds
ratios; the table labels them only "Estimate ( ± SE)" [Griffin et al. 2022, p. 8].

**Activity models.** Mean activity per fish for 27 fly-caught fish was modelled with linear models using the same four
structures and two air codings [Griffin et al. 2022, p. 8, Table A2]. The fight-time-only model had the lowest value
(10.076; R² = 0.140), with a fight time effect of z = 2.02, p = 0.055 [Griffin et al. 2022, p. 8]. Change over time
was tested with `lme4::lmer` mixed models on activity averaged into three-minute intervals as an ordinal factor, with
trial identity as a random effect and interactions of interval with fight time or air exposure [Griffin et al. 2022,
pp. 3–4]. None of the interactions was significant (for example, interval by treatment-level air, F(8, 124.04) = 0.63,
p = 0.75) [Griffin et al. 2022, p. 6]. Significant terms from ANOVA were followed by Tukey contrasts with
`multcomp::glht` [Griffin et al. 2022, p. 4]. Table A2 is printed with unequal column lengths, so the model to which
each value belongs should be checked against the original PDF before the values are reused.

**Software.** Analyses used RStudio 1.4.953 with significance set at p ≤ 0.05, and `rstatix`, `dplyr`, `lubridate`,
`data.table`, `ggplot2`, and `sjPlot` for comparisons, wrangling, and figures [Griffin et al. 2022, p. 3].

## Open questions

- Monitoring lasted 15 min, and the authors noted "there may be unknown delayed sub-lethal effects that could lead to
  post-release mortality days to weeks after release" [Griffin et al. 2022, p. 7].
- Blood physiology was not sampled, so the link between reflex scores, activity, and physiological disturbance in
  giant trevally remains untested.
- Statistical power was described as "relatively low with small sample sizes" [Griffin et al. 2022, p. 7], and air
  exposure stopped at 30 s; the trend toward impairment with air (p = 0.07) might resolve with longer exposures or
  larger groups.
- Conventional tackle, treble hooked poppers of 10–30 cm, and deeper reef fisheries were not tested [Griffin et al.
  2022, p. 7].
- Repeated capture is likely in a mandatory release fishery and "has unknown consequences on the animal's physiology
  and, in turn, their fitness" [Griffin et al. 2022, p. 7].
- The authors called for continued evaluation of accelerometer-derived activity "in other C&R oriented fisheries,
  especially for those that are known to be sensitive to angling events" [Griffin et al. 2022, p. 8].
